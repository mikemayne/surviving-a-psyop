# Victim Blaming

**Effects:** Increase shame, Undermine confidence, Increase distress.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Discourage reporting.

One possible psychological manipulation tactic is to reverse the roles of victim and abuser, making the person reporting mistreatment appear responsible for it.

Someone engaging in abusive behaviour might react strongly to being called an abuser, or object forcefully when the person describing their experiences identifies as a victim.

This can shift attention away from the alleged conduct and towards the supposed failings, credibility or behaviour of the person reporting it. The target may be encouraged to feel responsible for their own treatment, ashamed of their reactions, or pressured into defending their right to describe themselves as a victim.

Another variation is to portray the target's attempts to defend themselves, seek help or report mistreatment as evidence that they are the real aggressor.

**Don't accept responsibility for somebody else's behaviour, and don't get drawn into arguments about labels.**

Describe what happened, preserve relevant evidence and allow others to examine the facts. A forceful denial does not establish innocence, just as an accusation does not establish guilt.

You don't need somebody else's permission to describe your experiences or seek help.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
