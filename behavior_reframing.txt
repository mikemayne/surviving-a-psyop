# Behavior Reframing

Behavior reframing changes how an action is understood by assigning it a motive or meaning.

> “He’s trying to get fat to change his appearance.”

In this example, eating is recast as an attempt to change one’s appearance and avoid recognition. The insinuation takes guilt for granted. It invites people to consider *how* someone is supposedly evading justice while skipping over *what*, if anything, they’ve done. That can make an ordinary act feel shameful and put the person in the position of having to explain why they eat or look the way they do.
