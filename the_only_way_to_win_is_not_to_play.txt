# The Only Way to Win Is Not to Play

Typical psychological advice that you get from Google or AI models doesn't work when you're dealing with deliberate psychological abuse.

It generally involves trying to fix a broken relationship, explain your feelings, set boundaries, or maintain a sense of control by responding and engaging.

The problem is that this advice assumes you're dealing with someone acting in good faith.

There is an important difference between **good-faith actors who are potentially mistreating you, but can be corrected and helped, and bad-faith actors who are deliberately using weaponised psychology.**

A good-faith actor might be behaving badly because of a misunderstanding, poor judgement, or an emotional reaction. Talking to them might help. They may be willing to change their behaviour.

A bad-faith actor using psychological manipulation is different. Your attempts to reason with them, defend yourself, correct their statements, are all opportunities for them to further abuse you.

The engagement itself can be the point.

There is nothing you can say that will force someone who is deliberately abusing you to stop. Trying to persuade them gives them more of your time, attention, and emotional energy.

**There is only one effective strategy for refusing to participate in this manipulation: don't engage at all.**

Don't respond. Don't argue. Don't try to correct them. Don't try to make them understand how you feel.

You owe them nothing.

The only immediate response you should make to an abusers actions is to call the police if you feel threatened by them.

**The only way to win is not to play.**
