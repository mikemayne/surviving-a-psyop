# Weaponised Reporting

**Effects:** Increase fear, Increase hesitation, Undermine confidence.

**Possible objectives:** Discourage reporting, Intimidate through implied retaliation, Claim preferential treatment by authorities.

## The technique

**Example:** "It's only snitching when you do it."

The same action is presented as having completely different consequences depending on who does it.

- **When the victim reports:** It is called snitching, with the implication that criminals may retaliate.
- **When the abuser reports:** It is presented as legitimate, with the suggestion that the police will take their side.

This is not simply name-calling. It makes seeking help appear dangerous for one person and safe for the other.

## How it works

Calling a report "snitching" can make a person feel that asking for help is a betrayal carrying a risk of punishment. Claiming that the police will side with the other person adds a second pressure: the suggestion that reporting is futile because the outcome has already been decided.

Together, these messages try to discourage independent action while implying that the speaker can report without facing the same risks. A claim of police favouritism or criminal retaliation does not establish that either exists.

## Recognising the pattern

Look for a clear double standard about comparable reports, particularly when it is combined with a threat of retaliation or a claim of special protection. Keep the actual words separate from assumptions about what criminals or the police would do.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
