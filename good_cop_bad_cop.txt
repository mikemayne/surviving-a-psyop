### Good Cop / Bad Cop — Alternating Threats and Reassurance

**Effects:** Uncertainty, dependence.

**Possible objectives:** Encourage compliance.

**Good cop / bad cop** is a psychological manipulation technique in which two or more people adopt contrasting roles. One behaves aggressively, making threats, accusations or unreasonable demands, while another presents themselves as sympathetic, reasonable and potentially helpful. The objective is to make the victim more willing to trust and cooperate with the apparently benevolent individual, whose behaviour seems reassuring by comparison.

The technique exploits **contrast, fear, relief and dependency**. The aggressive individual creates distress and uncertainty, while the sympathetic individual offers an apparent escape from that distress. The victim may consequently become more receptive to suggestions, disclose information they would otherwise withhold, or agree to demands they would ordinarily reject.

The roles may alternate, with the sympathetic individual becoming hostile if the victim refuses to cooperate, or the aggressive individual suddenly becoming friendly. This creates an unpredictable emotional environment in which cooperation appears to be the only reliable way to obtain reassurance or avoid further hostility.

**A particularly manipulative variation involves presenting the sympathetic individual as someone secretly trying to protect the victim from the others.** They may claim to be taking personal risks by helping, encourage secrecy, or imply that their ability to intervene depends on the victim following instructions. This can create a false sense of loyalty and obligation.

The underlying objective is to **replace independent judgement with compliance motivated by fear and relief**. The apparent kindness of the sympathetic participant becomes part of the pressure rather than a genuine alternative to it.

**Countermeasure:** Judge each person's actions and requests independently rather than relative to the behaviour of others. Someone being less aggressive does not automatically make their demands reasonable or establish that they can be trusted. Maintain ordinary boundaries, avoid making decisions under pressure, and remember that the withdrawal of hostility is not a favour for which anything is owed.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
