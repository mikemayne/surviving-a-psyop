# Staged Third-Party Reactions

**Effects:** Create shame, uncertainty and fear of social judgement.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques.

### The technique

A conversation is presented as though a new person has just arrived and is being shown something concerning the target.

For example:

- First voice: "Have a look at this."
- Second voice: "How STUPID can you be?"

The target is left to imagine what the newcomer has supposedly been shown and why their reaction is so contemptuous.

### How it works

The technique combines several psychological mechanisms:

- **Social proof:** Creating the impression that another person independently shares a negative judgement.
- **Humiliation:** Encouraging the target to feel embarrassed or ashamed without even knowing what they supposedly did wrong.
- **Inviting speculation:** Leaving the subject of the conversation unexplained, encouraging the target to fill in the gaps.
- **Implied evidence:** Suggesting that damaging information exists without actually presenting any.

The apparent arrival of a new participant makes the reaction seem spontaneous and independent rather than rehearsed.

### Further considerations
Don't assume that the conversation concerns you, that a new person is actually involved, or that anything incriminating has been shown.

An unexplained expression of contempt provides no useful information. There is nothing to investigate or defend yourself against without something concrete to address.

**Don't fill in the blanks for them. Disengage and carry on with your day.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
