# Social Media

I don't use social media myself. But if you think you might be the subject of a psychological operation, my advice is simple: **stop using social media immediately.**

You don't need to work out whether anyone is deliberately targeting you before making that decision. Social media is an optional source of information and interaction. If it is leaving you feeling watched, threatened, angry or compelled to check what happens next, you can simply stop using it.

## Get Out of the Loop

Delete the apps from your phone, turn off notifications and stop checking the feeds. Don't post to see who reacts. Don't search for messages that might be about you. Don't keep going back to find out whether something strange is still happening.

Online feeds are designed to keep your attention. They can repeatedly show upsetting or oddly relevant material because of recommendation algorithms, ordinary coincidences or the way we notice things that concern us. None of that, on its own, establishes that somebody is conducting an operation against you.

The practical point is the same whatever the explanation: **you do not have to keep exposing yourself to it.**

## Stay Off Until Things Settle

My advice is to stay off social media while unusual or upsetting experiences are drawing you in. Give yourself a proper break and return your attention to people, activities and routines in ordinary life.

If things start to feel more settled, you can decide whether going back would actually improve your life. There is no obligation to return. Don't treat the break as an experiment designed to prove what caused anything; it is a way to protect your time, concentration and peace of mind.

Keep in touch with people you know and trust through direct contact if that works better for you. If you do eventually use social media again, restrict who can contact you and share only what you're comfortable making public.

If you receive a specific, credible threat, preserve the relevant message if convenient and report it through an appropriate channel. You do not need to monitor an account continuously to keep yourself safe. If distressing experiences continue beyond social media or begin affecting your sleep or daily life, consider discussing them with someone you trust or a health professional.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**You cannot get pulled into a social-media argument if you're no longer reading the feed.**
