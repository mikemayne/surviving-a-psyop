**Narrating — Surveillance Intimidation and Behavioural Conditioning**

**Technique:** Narrating involves repeatedly describing or repeating an individual's words, actions or ordinary behaviour aloud, creating the impression that they are being continuously watched and monitored. This can be combined with sudden loud bangs or other unpleasant noises, timed to coincide with behaviour the abuser disapproves of.

**Psychological objective:** The purpose is to make the individual feel that even their most mundane actions are subject to constant scrutiny and judgement. The loud noises serve as apparent signals of disapproval, encouraging the individual to modify their behaviour to avoid further unpleasant interruptions. Over time, this can produce hypervigilance, self-censorship and a diminished sense of privacy, even when alone.

**Psychological mechanism:** The technique exploits *aversive conditioning* and *associative learning*. Repeatedly pairing particular behaviours with unpleasant stimuli can encourage someone to anticipate those stimuli and alter their actions accordingly. Narration additionally creates a feeling of being observed, potentially encouraging the individual to internalise the apparent scrutiny and begin policing their own behaviour. The combination attempts to turn ordinary daily activities into a source of stress and uncertainty.

An important feature is that **the psychological effect depends on the individual's perception of monitoring, not necessarily on the existence of continuous surveillance**.
