DON'T TOLERATE MISTREATMENT — REPORT IT CALMLY AND FAIRLY

Disengaging from provocation does not mean accepting mistreatment. If someone behaves abusively, acts unprofessionally, makes threats or fails to fulfil their responsibilities, you have every right to raise the matter through appropriate channels. Simply tolerating repeated misconduct can allow it to continue or escalate. The objective is to challenge unacceptable behaviour without allowing it to dictate your own.

**Stay calm, report fairly, and give everyone a reasonable opportunity to explain their actions.** Describe what happened, what was said, when it occurred and why you consider it unacceptable. Avoid exaggeration, speculation or personal attacks. If an explanation is offered, consider it fairly, even if you initially find it unsatisfactory. There may be circumstances you are unaware of, and an apparent failure may have a perfectly ordinary explanation.

If you do not receive an adequate response, **escalate the matter politely to the next appropriate person in the chain of responsibility.** Do not assume that someone who fails to help you is deliberately obstructing you or participating in a wider conspiracy. They may simply be busy, overworked, inexperienced, poorly informed, incompetent or operating within an inadequate system. It may feel personal, particularly after repeated disappointments, but attributing malicious intentions without evidence rarely improves your chances of obtaining a useful response.

Keep written communications factual, concise and courteous. Explain what remains unanswered, what reasonable action you are requesting, and why the matter warrants further attention. Preserve correspondence so that anyone reviewing the complaint can see exactly how it has been handled. If necessary, seek independent advice or use a formal complaints or regulatory process rather than endlessly repeating the same arguments.

Frustration and anger are entirely understandable responses to genuine mistreatment, but formal correspondence is rarely the best place to express them. **Vent those feelings privately to trusted friends or supportive professionals, and let the facts and written record speak for themselves.** A calm, persistent and proportionate approach protects your credibility, makes it easier for others to assist you, and reduces the likelihood of unnecessary conflict.

**The central principle is that you can refuse to tolerate mistreatment without becoming confrontational. Be reasonable, be persistent, give people the benefit of the doubt where appropriate, and hold them accountable for what they actually do rather than what you suspect they might intend.**
