The Streisand Effect

The Streisand effect is a phenomenon in which an attempt to hide, censor or suppress information inadvertently attracts greater public attention to that information.

It is named after Barbra Streisand, whose 2003 lawsuit seeking to prevent publication of a photograph of her home brought considerably more attention to the photograph than it would otherwise have received.

Attempts to intimidate someone into silence, suppress complaints or prevent information from being discussed can sometimes have the opposite of their intended effect. The suppression itself may become a matter of public interest.

However, attempts to suppress information do not establish that the information is accurate, or that the person attempting suppression has acted improperly.

There is no need to deliberately provoke this effect. Doing so would contradict the principle of avoiding unnecessary engagement and escalation.

Instead, remain calm, document relevant events accurately, use appropriate reporting channels and allow others to draw their own conclusions from the available evidence.

The principle to remember is this: having more publicly documented evidence with police, health services etc makes it less likely for the abuser to want to draw attention to it. It's important that this documented evidence remains grounded, calm, non-threatening, non-accusatory. Don't write or say anything that you wouldn't want to be made public. You can use AI models connected to a separate email address to check your rights, what laws might be being broken, and even write a calm email on your behalf. Make sure you check any legal advice with a solicitor before relying on it however.
