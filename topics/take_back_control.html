Take back control

The most satisfying thing is knowing when they've failed to manipulate you. They hate it :)

I found things generally got easier when I learnt to disengage completely, and developed successful strategies for ignoring their attempts at manipulation. Things got so bad for me pychologically that I was unable to complain successfully for several year, primarily because I didn't complain soon enough and allow things to go to far.

My advice is complain soon and often, but remain fair, assume people are acting in good faith and give everyone a change. Don't assume that someone is out to get you just because they have a bad day at the office.

I found in general as soon as something was documented somewhere it stopped happening, either with a doctor or the police. They will try to manipulate you to think that you owe them secrecy. You owe them nothing. Conversely, beware of them actively tring to get you to share information that is innaccurate, based on something they said rather than a provable fact and through the improper channel. Always start at the bottom, give that person a fair chance to deal with the issue, only then escalate.

I think its fair to demand politely certain things of professionals. A direct question either deserves an answer, a referral or a valid reason not to be answered. If your questions remain unanswered, point that out politely then escalate without arguing or taking it personally. Exercising your rights should never be treated as an inconvenience, even if the professional doesn't necessarily know why you need to do it. You don't need to explain why you are exercising your rights, you just need to say that you want to. Equally professionals deserve fair, polite treatment. If your mental state makes this difficult, consider explaining your situation to AI, a friend or a solicitor and having them help you draft a calm, collected email.

Remember you don't owe a stranger an explanation for anything, especially one who is trying to coerce you. Politely decline and walk away. If they behave aggressively, keep walking. Consider calling 999 if you feel in danger. Don't fall into the trap of getting into an unnecessary confrontation.

Learn to recognise a call to action. If someone puts an arbitry time limit on something, accuses you of taking too long to answer a question or tries to get you to answer a question without thinking it through, its possible they are acting in bad faith.

You can't control what they try to put in your mind and you can't help but engage with it. But with practice you can control the nature of the engagement. Instead of thinkng of the Pink Elephant you can learn to think "Why does he want me to think about a pink elephant?". This satisfies the human need to address a comment without producing the same emotional response. And you can always control your response.

If you're not sure what to do, just don't do anything.
