Criminalising Normal Bodily Functions

**Objective:** To make the victim feel that even their involuntary bodily functions can constitute criminal behaviour, moral wrongdoing or non-compliance with an imposed set of rules. This creates an impossible standard of behaviour and encourages constant self-monitoring, shame and anxiety.

**Method:** Ordinary bodily functions such as breathing, swallowing, coughing, sneezing, flatulence, urination, defecation or involuntary sexual responses are assigned arbitrary meanings. The victim may be told that a particular bodily function constitutes a sex crime, a hate crime, an insult, an admission of guilt or a breach of previously imposed instructions.

Surveillance technology may be incorporated into this strategy. Cameras, microphones and specialised sensors can measure or infer certain physiological activities, including movement, breathing patterns and some bodily sounds. Where such monitoring is available, observations can be selectively interpreted as evidence of deliberate wrongdoing, even when the behaviour is entirely ordinary or involuntary.

**Psychological mechanism:** The technique exploits embarrassment, disgust, social taboos and the natural human desire to control how one is perceived. By treating unavoidable biological processes as deliberate moral choices, the abuser creates a situation in which compliance is literally impossible.

The resulting self-consciousness can itself increase the frequency of some physiological responses, creating a self-reinforcing cycle of attention, discomfort and further accusations.

**Defence:** Recognise the distinction between voluntary behaviour and involuntary biological processes. An arbitrary accusation does not turn a normal bodily function into a crime or a meaningful breach of instructions. There is no reason to modify ordinary bodily behaviour to satisfy invented rules.
