Practical advice for checking assumptions, considering simpler explanations, and separating direct experience from interpretation.

Staying Grounded

The simplest advice is not to engage. But curiosity can get the better of you, particularly when something unusual or distressing has happened.

Feel free to explore whatever hypotheticals you like, but never confuse a plausible explanation with a proven one.

When considering extraordinary technical capabilities, secret legal processes or elaborate psychological techniques, always look for simpler explanations first. Consider ordinary, proven technologies and established facts before adopting increasingly improbable theories.

If you find yourself considering increasingly extraordinary explanations, and hear someone loudly proclaiming "He's figured it out!", "He knows everything!" or something similar, remember that you only hear what is being said, not necessarily why it is being said. Don't mistake apparent confirmation for evidence that your theory is correct.

Separate Experience From Explanation

Don't allow your direct experiences to be dismissed simply because you cannot explain them. But distinguish what you experienced from what you believe caused it.

Be willing to consider alternative interpretations, examine contradictory evidence and abandon explanations that don't stand up to scrutiny.

Know When to Stop

My advice is to disengage and do something else. Go for a walk, read a book, spend time with friends or get some sleep.

If you feel compelled to continue, return to two simple questions:

What do I actually know, and what am I assuming?

Is there a simpler, more grounded explanation?

You don't have to explain everything. It's perfectly reasonable to say "I don't know" and move on.
