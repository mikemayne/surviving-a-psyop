Greater-Good Moral Blackmail

**Objective**

The objective of this manipulation strategy is to prevent an individual from exposing abusive or unethical conduct by convincing them that doing so would jeopardise important, beneficial work. The individual is made to feel that protecting others, society, or even national security requires them to tolerate mistreatment and remain silent about it.

**Psychological Techniques**

The strategy exploits **empathy, moral responsibility, guilt and altruism**. It creates a false moral dilemma in which the individual must supposedly choose between protecting themselves and protecting the wider public. It may also involve *moral licensing*, where good deeds are presented as justification for harmful conduct, and *noble-cause corruption*, where unethical methods are defended by reference to supposedly worthwhile objectives.

**Mechanism**

Responsibility is deliberately inverted. Instead of the person engaging in abusive conduct being responsible for their actions, the person experiencing the abuse is made to feel responsible for the consequences of revealing it. Any objection can be reframed as selfishness, disloyalty or disregard for the greater good. The claimed importance of the work also discourages scrutiny of whether the harmful conduct is necessary, proportionate or justified.

**Countermeasure**

Separate the claimed benefits of an activity from the methods used to carry it out. Even genuinely valuable work does not automatically justify abuse, nor does exposing misconduct necessarily require legitimate work to stop. Assess each claim independently, rather than accepting the proposed moral dilemma. A simple guiding principle is that **responsibility for abusive conduct belongs to those engaging in it, not to the person who objects to it**.
