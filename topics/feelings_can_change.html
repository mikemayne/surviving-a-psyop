Look for Evidence, and Remember That Feelings Can Change

Two useful psychological principles are question your assumptions and remember that emotional responses are not necessarily permanent.

When something frightening, upsetting or confusing happens, try to distinguish what you know from what you suspect. Look for evidence rather than automatically accepting either your own interpretation or somebody else's assertions. It is perfectly reasonable to conclude that you don't yet know what something means. Don't make assumptions about peoples opinions of you, within reason take the things they say at face value and judge them on their actions and behavior rather than what you have been directly told by an abuser or how you may instinctively feel. At the same time its quite reasonable to examine how their statements make you feel, and question whether they are trying to manipulate you. Always behave ethically and legally and question the motives of anyone who regularly attempts to get you to behave otherwise.

The second principle offers hope: our emotional responses can change with time and experience. A situation that feels overwhelming today may not provoke the same reaction indefinitely. This is related to the principles behind exposure therapy, a recognised psychological treatment in which people gradually learn to manage their responses to feared situations. However, exposure therapy is a carefully structured approach, not a reason to tolerate mistreatment, deliberately place yourself in danger or attempt to treat psychological trauma without appropriate support. You don't have to solve every mystery, and you don't have to remain frightened forever. If distress is affecting your life, consider speaking with a qualified professional. The [NHS provides information about talking therapies and how to access them](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/talking-therapies/), including free self-referral services in England. The mental health charity [Mind explains exposure therapy and the importance of a safe, controlled approach](https://www.mind.org.uk/information-support/types-of-mental-health-problems/phobias/coping-with-phobias/).
