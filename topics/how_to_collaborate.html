How to collaborate

You are welcome to suggest a correction, practical advice or an anonymised account of an experience that may help other readers. Sharing is entirely optional. You don't need any technical knowledge. The ChatGPT link below can help you draft your contribution in ordinary language and, only if you decide to submit it, guide you through the process. You can write a draft without creating an account or publishing anything.

Health, safety and professional support. If you feel unwell, consider contacting your GP or a qualified mental health professional. If you feel unsafe, contact the police (999 in an emergency). This project is not a legal, medical, mental health or evidence-reporting service. I cannot and will not help with legal or health issues, or take a position on anyone's mental health or legal status. Specific incidents needing professional assessment or investigation, and any video or audio recordings, belong with an appropriate qualified health professional or the police, as applicable — not here.

What I will consider. Submissions must be anonymised and strictly ordinary plain text in a `.txt` file. No Markdown files, PDFs, Word documents, images, video, audio, HTML, JavaScript, scripts, code, other attachments or formats. Describe what happened separately from what you think may have caused it, and avoid presenting assumptions about other people's motives as established facts. Do not submit raw evidence or private records. Contributions offered for inclusion are under [CC0 1.0 Universal](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).

Protect everyone's privacy. Remove names and identifying details about yourself and others, including workplaces, schools, exact dates and places, usernames, links and distinctive combinations of facts. ChatGPT can help check a draft, but it can miss identifying clues; review it yourself before sharing. The link requests Temporary Chat, but check that the conversation is actually marked Temporary before entering anything personal. Temporary Chat is not an anonymous or confidential reporting service, and a copy may be retained for up to 30 days for safety reasons. Avoid sharing unnecessary personal details even while drafting.

Know what becomes public. A GitHub submission is publicly visible as soon as it is submitted, even if I reject it. Your account name, public fork, proposed text, submission discussion and commit history may be visible and may remain in archives after deletion. Consider a separate pseudonymous GitHub account and email address, keep your email private or use GitHub's no-reply address for commits, and check the file, account and author details before authorising anything public. These steps reduce exposure but cannot guarantee anonymity. You are free to keep your draft private.

Review and responses. An automated check flags submissions that break the file-format rules. Nothing is added to the book automatically; I will personally review submissions before accepting them. Under these strict conditions, I will attempt to consider all submissions eventually, but cannot guarantee a response.
