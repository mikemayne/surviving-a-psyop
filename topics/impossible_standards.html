Impossible Standards, Relentless Criticism and Moral Superiority

**Objective**

To undermine an individual's confidence, self-worth and independence by subjecting them to constant, unreasonable criticism while presenting the critic as morally, intellectually or professionally superior. The individual is placed in a position where nothing they do is ever considered adequate, and any attempt to defend themselves becomes further grounds for criticism.

**Psychological Techniques**

This combines perfectionism-based manipulation, moving the goalposts, double standards, chronic invalidation and moral superiority. It may also involve *double binds*, where every available course of action attracts criticism, and *moral grandstanding*, where the critic repeatedly emphasises their supposedly exceptional standards to establish authority over the individual.

**Mechanism**

The individual is subjected to relentless scrutiny of their words, decisions, behaviour and emotional responses. Ordinary mistakes are exaggerated, reasonable actions are portrayed as inadequate, and contradictory expectations may be imposed so that satisfying one demand creates grounds for another criticism.

The critic simultaneously claims to hold themselves to exceptionally high standards, establishing an artificial moral hierarchy in which they occupy the superior position. Their own conduct, however, may be judged by entirely different criteria or excused by reference to their intentions, responsibilities or supposedly important objectives.

Over time, this can encourage excessive self-monitoring, self-doubt and a constant need to justify even trivial decisions. The individual may become preoccupied with avoiding criticism rather than exercising independent judgement.

The technique is particularly insidious when the critic's own behaviour would fail the standards they impose on others. The contradiction is not necessarily acknowledged; instead, the individual may be criticised for noticing or questioning it.

**Countermeasure**

Evaluate criticism against reasonable, consistent and independently applicable standards rather than accepting the critic's claimed authority. Distinguish constructive feedback, which identifies specific problems and achievable improvements, from criticism designed to make satisfactory performance impossible.

Avoid becoming trapped in endless attempts to satisfy arbitrary or constantly changing demands. Assess your own behaviour according to your values, ordinary social expectations and the same standards you would reasonably apply to others.

**A person claiming exceptionally high moral standards is not exempt from being judged by those same standards.**
