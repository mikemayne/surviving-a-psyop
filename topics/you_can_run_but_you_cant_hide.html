You Can Run, But You Can't Hide

"You can run, but you can't hide" is a threatening expression that presents a false choice. It implies that your only options are to flee or conceal yourself, and that neither will work.

Don't accept the premise.

You don't have to run. You don't have to hide. You can go wherever you like, carry on with your life, and make your own decisions.

A related technique is to claim that ordinary behaviour makes you appear guilty.

Examples include:

  • "If you leave, you'll look guilty."
  • "If you refuse to answer, they'll think you've got something to hide."
  • "If you contact the police, you'll make things worse."
  • "If you speak to a solicitor, that makes you look guilty."
  • "An innocent person wouldn't behave like that."

These statements attempt to make you judge your actions through the eyes of an imagined observer, rather than according to your own interests, rights and judgement.

They may also create a double bind, in which every possible response is presented as evidence of guilt.

Don't accept legal advice from someone who is threatening, intimidating or manipulating you. They have no authority to determine what makes you look guilty, what your rights are, or how you should behave.

If you genuinely need legal advice, obtain it independently from a qualified professional.

The best response is to continue making ordinary, lawful decisions based on what is right for you, not on the meanings somebody else tries to attach to them.

You do not have to prove your innocence by behaving according to somebody else's rules.
