Manipulated Media — Deepfakes and Fabricated Interactions

THE TECHNIQUE

Manipulated media involves using altered, fabricated or deliberately staged audiovisual content to create misleading impressions, provoke emotional reactions or influence an individual's behaviour.

Examples include AI-generated deepfakes, altered television broadcasts, prerecorded material presented as live, and genuinely produced livestreams in which participants deliberately perform scripted interactions.

A deepfake may digitally alter someone's appearance, voice or speech, while a genuine livestream could achieve a similar psychological effect through performers following instructions. The resulting content need not be technically sophisticated to be convincing.

In a hypothetical targeted manipulation, the apparent communication could range from extremely subtle remarks to explicit statements seemingly addressed to the viewer.

This could be combined with the **“Talking blag”**, in which an individual is encouraged to interpret indirect or ambiguous speech as a form of covert communication.

Where the individual engages with this supposed covert conversation, subtle remarks or apparent responses might be sufficient to sustain the impression of an interaction. If the individual refuses to engage, the material could become increasingly explicit, potentially progressing to someone appearing to address them directly.

WHY IT WORKS

People generally assume that audiovisual material is a reasonably faithful representation of something that actually happened.

Live broadcasts can be particularly persuasive because they appear spontaneous, immediate and difficult to fabricate.

Modern synthetic media challenges these assumptions. Real-time face replacement, voice synthesis and other techniques can produce convincing audiovisual content, while staged genuine broadcasts can create misleading impressions without requiring any AI technology.

The psychological mechanism can also exploit **personal relevance and perceived interactivity**. A remark that seems unusually relevant to someone's circumstances may attract their attention, especially when it follows a subject they have recently been thinking about or discussing.

If the viewer begins treating ordinary dialogue as a coded exchange, subsequent ambiguous statements may appear to be responses. This interpretation can become self-reinforcing, even where no actual communication exists.

More explicit material could, in principle, be used to provoke surprise, fear, anger or confusion, particularly if it appeared to contradict the viewer's understanding of what was happening.

RECOGNISING THE PATTERN

Manipulated audiovisual content can take many forms, including fabricated news reports, impersonated public figures, edited conversations, staged livestreams and apparently spontaneous interactions.

Potential warning signs include inconsistent audiovisual details, suspicious source information, discrepancies between independent broadcasts, or content that cannot be corroborated elsewhere.

However, technical manipulation is not always visually detectable, and unusual or personally relevant remarks are not themselves reliable evidence of fabrication or targeting.

A particularly important distinction is between **what the media demonstrably contains and what the viewer interprets it to mean**.

A person appearing to look into a camera, make a relevant remark or address an unspecified audience does not, by itself, establish that they are communicating with a particular viewer.

PRACTICAL RESPONSE

Avoid treating apparently personalised audiovisual content as an instruction or invitation to interact.

Do not engage in supposed covert conversations with television programmes, livestreams or other media. If something appears to address you personally, consider its ordinary context before assigning it a more elaborate meaning.

Where authenticity matters, compare the material with an independent source, such as an official broadcast archive or a separate verified livestream. Preserve original recordings and source information if there is a concrete reason to investigate suspected manipulation.

Avoid repeatedly analysing ambiguous dialogue in an attempt to decode hidden meanings. This can increase attention to coincidences without establishing whether any manipulation occurred.

If content is disturbing, disengage from it and return to ordinary activities. There is no obligation to respond to anything appearing on a screen.

**The central principle is that audiovisual realism does not guarantee authenticity, but apparent personal relevance does not establish deliberate communication. Independent verification is more useful than attempting to interpret ambiguous material as a coded exchange.**
