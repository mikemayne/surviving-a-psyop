# Suicide Coercion — Threats of Indefinite Abuse

**This is a potentially very harmful pattern.** Repeated intimidation, humiliation or threats can have serious psychological effects. If you are experiencing something like this, consider contacting a medical professional to discuss your experiences and concerns. You do not need to react immediately or deal with this alone.

**Effects:** Increase fear, Increase distress, Undermine confidence.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Induce self-harm.

### THE TECHNIQUE

Suicide coercion involves using threats, humiliation, intimidation or sustained psychological pressure to encourage an individual to take their own life. An especially cruel variation is to present suicide as the only possible means of escaping ongoing abuse.

Statements such as **“This goes on until you kill yourself”** combine an explicit reference to suicide with the assertion that the suffering will continue indefinitely.

The objective is to convince the individual that resistance is futile, that nobody will intervene, and that there is no realistic prospect of their circumstances improving. The threatened continuation of abuse becomes a means of attempting to control the individual's decisions and undermine their instinct for self-preservation.

### WHY IT WORKS

The technique exploits the psychological effects of entrapment, hopelessness and learned helplessness.

Human beings can endure considerable hardship when they believe it is temporary or that they have some means of changing their circumstances. Presenting suffering as permanent, inescapable and entirely outside their control can undermine that resilience.

Sleep deprivation, social isolation, repeated humiliation and attacks on the individual's reputation may compound the effect by weakening emotional resilience and reducing access to supportive relationships.

A particularly dangerous element is the creation of a false choice: either continue suffering indefinitely or end your own life. The manipulation depends on convincing the individual that these are their only options.

### RECOGNISING THE PATTERN

The distinguishing feature is the deliberate presentation of suicide as an expected, desirable or inevitable outcome.

This may involve explicit statements encouraging suicide, repeated assertions that the individual has no future, claims that nobody will help them, or threats that mistreatment will continue until they die.

The technique may also involve alternating periods of intimidation with promises that the suffering is almost over, only to withdraw those promises and resume the pressure.

The intended psychological effect is not simply fear. It is the destruction of hope and the individual's belief in their ability to influence their own future.

### Further considerations
**Never accept an abuser's claimed authority over whether your life is worth living.**

A threat that suffering will continue forever is a statement intended to influence your expectations, not a reliable prediction of the future.

Do not attempt to negotiate an end to mistreatment by harming yourself, and do not treat demands involving self-harm as legitimate instructions.

Preserve specific threatening statements where possible, disengage from abusive interactions, and seek independent support from people who are not involved in the situation.

If sustained psychological pressure is affecting your ability to think clearly or remain safe, prioritise sleep, ordinary routines, supportive human contact and professional assistance.

If you feel at immediate risk of acting on suicidal thoughts, contact emergency services or attend an emergency department. In the UK, Samaritans can also be contacted on **116 123**, free, at any time.

**The central principle is that no person, institution or supposed authority has the right to demand your death, and no threat of indefinite suffering should be accepted as an accurate description of your future.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
