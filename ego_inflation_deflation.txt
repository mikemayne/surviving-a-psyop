# Ego Inflation and Deflation

**Effects:** Overconfidence or self-doubt.

**Possible objectives:** Encourage impulsive choices or compliance.

## The Pattern

Two short remarks can push a person's self-image in opposite directions:

- **Ego inflation:** “Who the fuck is this guy?”
- **Ego deflation:** “Who does he think he is?”

The first can sound like astonishment at someone's importance, abilities or status. The second challenges the person's right to think highly of themselves. If such remarks are deliberately used to influence a listener, one may encourage an inflated self-image while the other may provoke insecurity or a desire to prove themselves.

Neither sentence establishes its speaker's intention. Both can also be ordinary expressions of surprise, irritation, disbelief or curiosity, depending on the context.

## How It Works

**Inflation through implied importance.** “Who the fuck is this guy?” contains no explicit compliment, but a listener who takes it as admiration may supply their own flattering explanation: perhaps they are exceptional, powerful or destined for something extraordinary. That interpretation can be more persuasive than direct praise because it feels self-discovered. If taken seriously, it may contribute to overconfidence, grandiose thinking or impulsive choices.

**Deflation through implied arrogance.** “Who does he think he is?” suggests someone is overstepping, acting above their station or claiming status they have not earned. A listener who applies it to themselves may feel embarrassed or defensive, or want to demonstrate their worth. That reaction can make approval and social standing feel more important than the actual decision at hand.

**Alternating up and down.** Where exaggerated approval and dismissal are used together, the contrast can make someone's attention drift towards how others supposedly perceive them. Both approaches can invite a response: acting out a newly inflated identity, or trying to disprove a negative judgement.

## Recognising the Pattern

The useful distinction is **self-image rather than outcomes**. “He's going to be a millionaire” predicts a future result; “Who the fuck is this guy?” can invite a story about who a person *is*. “Nobody will use that” dismisses an outcome; “Who does he think he is?” can undermine a person's sense of standing.

An overheard sentence is especially easy to misinterpret because its subject, tone and surrounding conversation may be unknown. There is no need to decide whether it concerned you or was intended to be heard. The practical question is whether you're allowing an unsupported impression of someone else's opinion to influence your choices.

Grandiose thinking and clinical mania are not interchangeable. A provocative remark is not, by itself, a basis for concluding that someone is experiencing or being pushed into a manic episode.

## Further Considerations

Neither apparent admiration nor contempt deserves control over your decisions. Base important choices on your own values, concrete evidence, appropriate advice and a realistic understanding of the risks. Postpone impulsive commitments, particularly when stressed or short of sleep.

If an example is worth documenting, note the exact words and circumstances without turning an interpretation into a fact. You need not analyse every passing comment.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
