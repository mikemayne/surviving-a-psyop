# The Imaginary Line

**Effects:** Increase fear, Increase distress, Increase emotional reactivity.

**Possible objectives:** Provoke a reaction, Induce compliance, Increase the effectiveness of other techniques.

## The Technique

Pretend the victim has just crossed some imaginary line, and that there will now be serious, unspecified consequences.

The statement might be as simple as:

- "Right, THAT'S IT!"
- "You've done it now."
- "Now you're for it."
- "That's the last straw."
- "You've gone too far this time."
- "You'll regret that."

Nobody explains what the boundary was, what the victim supposedly did wrong, or what the consequences will be.

## How It Works

The trick is to make the victim think something significant has just happened, without actually telling them anything.

The victim may start thinking about what they've just said or done, wondering whether they've made a mistake or whether something unpleasant is about to happen.

The lack of specifics is important. **The victim is left to imagine the consequences themselves.**

The statement can also be used to grab the victim's attention, interrupt what they're doing, or provoke them into asking what they've supposedly done wrong.

The intended effect is to create apprehension and uncertainty without needing to make a specific threat.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
