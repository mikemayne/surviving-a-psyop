# Manufactured Moral Outrage — "How Very Dare You?"

**Effects:** Increase shame, Increase self-consciousness, Increase emotional reactivity.

**Possible objectives:** Make ordinary behaviour appear incriminating, Encourage unjustified moral inferences, Provoke a defensive reaction.

## The pattern

An ordinary action is described with exaggerated shock or moral disapproval, as though the audience should already know why it is scandalous.

For example:

- "He's *really* reading that book?"
- "She *actually* said that?"
- "He's *really* listening to Nirvana?" — followed by a reference to the title of a controversial song.

The speaker may never make an explicit accusation. Instead, the tone supplies a "How very dare you!" reaction, inviting listeners to connect an ordinary choice with something offensive. A book, song or quotation may contain disturbing material; encountering it does not establish the reader's or listener's beliefs, character or actions.

## How it works

**Ordinary action → loaded association → performed outrage → implied wrongdoing.**

**Associative insinuation:** A person's harmless action is linked to a disturbing subject, without showing any relevant connection.

**Outrage in place of an argument:** Disbelief, emphasis or disgust makes the association feel important while leaving the actual criticism unstated.

**Pressure to explain:** If the remark is interpreted as an accusation, the person may feel compelled to defend something they have not done or a belief they have not expressed.

This differs from a direct moral accusation: the alleged offence is left for the listener to infer. It also differs from manufactured agreement, which explicitly treats consuming media as endorsing its content.

A dramatic reaction is not evidence of wrongdoing. Nor does an ambiguous remark, especially one overheard without context, establish that it was aimed at any particular person.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).
