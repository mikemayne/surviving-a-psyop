# Listening In

A lot of psychological manipulation techniques can make you paranoid. This might make you want to listen in on what's going on around you.

**This is the opposite of what you should do.**

You might find yourself listening for conversations through a wall, trying to make out what strangers are saying in a supermarket, or paying particular attention when you think you've heard your name, or something that was previously used to abuse you.  Using a word or phrase that relates to something that has been used to abuse you but sounds innocuous or at most weird to other people is a very effective way to attract your attention, make you feel paranoid and further draw you in.

Other ways to draw you in include staging fake phone calls for you to overhear, sometimes saying things like "It's not working, do you have any more things to try", loudly confronting each other in a neighbouring property saying things like "You said this would be over by now", in general discussing something apparently concerning you within earshot, or make it sound as though one team of people are handing over to another people summing up all the accusations they have made during the day as if they were fact. The intention is to attract your attention, and draw you in for further abuse.

Your natural instinct will be to try to hear more, and listen harder. But once you start listening out for it, you can become increasingly watchful. You notice more fragments, try to put them together, and become distracted from whatever you were doing.  This can push you towards hypervigillence and paranoia, and can make you appear to be behaving suspiciously.  This completely natural behavior can be used as evidence of wrong doing, to turn more people against you.

There is some straightforward psychology behind this. Hypervigilance makes you monitor your surroundings for possible threats. Selective attention makes personally relevant words, including your own name, stand out. When you can only partly hear a conversation, your expectations can influence how you interpret it. The abuser only has to provide enough context to make you consider it, and you are left to fill in the blanks yourself.  If you are already paranoid, the interpretation you come up with will often be the one that is the most terrifying to you.

**Assume any overheard information that sounds like it's about you is staged and ignore it.**

You don't need to work out whether a conversation was staged, what the speakers meant, or whether it had anything to do with you.  If they actually had any information about you they would be going to the police, not staging elaborate psychological abuse.

**Don't listen in. Don't try to piece conversations together. Don't confront anyone about an overheard fragment. Carry on with what you were doing.**

If someone needs to tell you something important, they can tell you directly. If you encounter an immediate, clear threat to safety, act to protect yourself and call **999**.

**don't give overheard conversations your attention.**
