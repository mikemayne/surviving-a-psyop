# Listening In

A lot of psychological manipulation techniques can make you paranoid. This might make you want to listen in on what's going on around you.

**This is the opposite of what you should do.**

You might find yourself listening for conversations through a wall, trying to make out what strangers are saying in a supermarket, or paying particular attention when you think you've heard your name.

If somebody wanted to draw you in, they could stage fake phone calls, loudly confront each other in a neighbouring property, discuss something apparently concerning you within earshot, or make it sound as though different people were handing over to one another. The immediate purpose would be to make you stop what you're doing and listen.

The natural instinct is to try to hear more, especially if something sounds like it's about you. But once you start listening out for it, you can become increasingly watchful. You notice more fragments, try to put them together, and become distracted from whatever you were doing.

There is some straightforward psychology behind this. Hypervigilance makes you monitor your surroundings for possible threats. Selective attention makes personally relevant words, including your own name, stand out. When you can only partly hear a conversation, your expectations can influence how you interpret it.

**Assume any overheard information that sounds like it's about you is staged and ignore it.**

Treat that as a practical rule for your attention, not something you need to investigate or prove. You don't need to work out whether a conversation was staged, what the speakers meant, or whether it had anything to do with you.

**Don't listen in. Don't try to piece conversations together. Don't confront anyone about an overheard fragment. Carry on with what you were doing.**

If someone needs to tell you something important, they can tell you directly. If you encounter an immediate, clear threat to safety, act to protect yourself and call **999**.

The point is simple: **don't give overheard conversations control over your attention.**
