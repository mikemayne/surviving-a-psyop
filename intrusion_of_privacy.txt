# Intrusion of Privacy

**Effects:** Make someone feel monitored, increase hypervigilance, self-consciousness and concern about privacy.

**Possible objectives:** Make ordinary private activities feel subject to scrutiny, encourage self-monitoring and distract someone from getting on with their life.

## The technique

Intrusion of privacy involves commenting on or criticising someone's personal choices in a way that makes them feel monitored.

Examples include remarks about how they spend their money, what they buy, what they eat, who they spend time with, how they relax or how they organise their day. These are ordinary private matters that may be none of the speaker's business.

Someone might criticise a purchase, make a judgement about how much money was spent or comment on a personal habit. The criticism is not necessarily the main point. It draws attention to something private and can leave the individual wondering how somebody else came to know about it.

A remark such as "Oh, you idiot!" can suggest that someone is watching what you're doing and is pleased because you're making an unwise decision. The supposed mistake is never identified. You're left wondering what you've done wrong and what consequences might follow. This can make you question ordinary decisions and reinforce the feeling that you're being monitored.

**The effect is to make you feel that your private life is being watched and judged.**

## How it works

- **Implied observation:** A remark about a personal decision can give the impression that somebody has been keeping track of what you do.
- **Loss of privacy:** Ordinary activities begin to feel exposed, even when you are alone.
- **Hypervigilance:** You may start looking for signs that other people know what you have been doing.
- **Self-monitoring:** You begin considering how your ordinary choices might be viewed or criticised instead of simply making them.
- **Guilt and shame:** Criticism of harmless spending or personal habits can make ordinary decisions feel wrong.
- **Distraction:** Time and attention that should be spent enjoying your life can instead be spent worrying about who might know what.

The comments do not need to contain threats. Even an apparently trivial remark can make a private activity feel less private.

## The effect

The central point is not that you are being asked to justify a purchase or explain a personal choice. **It is that you start to feel monitored.**

Repeated comments can extend that feeling beyond the particular activity being discussed. A remark about spending money can make someone more conscious of their shopping, their phone, their home and other parts of their everyday life. They may start changing habits, checking their surroundings or thinking about where information could have come from.

A feeling of being monitored is not proof that monitoring occurred. The psychological effect comes from the sense of scrutiny, and it can persist without an explanation for the comments.

**You should be able to make ordinary private decisions without organising your life around what somebody else might say about them.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
