# Intrusion of Privacy

**Effects:** Increase guilt, shame, self-consciousness and pressure to justify ordinary decisions.

**Possible objectives:** Erode autonomy, encourage self-monitoring, provoke defensive explanations and establish a sense of authority over private choices.

## The technique

The technique involves criticising someone's private decisions as though they are entitled to an explanation.

Examples include remarks about how much money someone spends, what they buy, what they eat, who they spend time with, how they relax or how they organise their day. These are ordinary personal choices that may be none of the speaker's business.

The criticism may be presented as concern, moral judgement or simple disapproval. The important part is the assumption that the speaker has a right to question the decision.

Someone might criticise you for buying something you enjoy, spending money on a hobby or choosing to spend an evening doing nothing. You are then invited to defend a choice that did not require anyone else's permission.

## How it works

- **Boundary crossing:** The speaker treats private decisions as matters they are entitled to judge.
- **Manufactured guilt:** An ordinary choice is presented as selfish, irresponsible or wasteful.
- **Forced justification:** The individual feels compelled to explain how they spend their money or time.
- **Erosion of autonomy:** Repeated criticism can make someone feel they need approval before making ordinary decisions.
- **Self-monitoring:** Someone may begin reconsidering harmless choices in anticipation of criticism.
- **Provocation:** Intrusive remarks can produce an angry response, diverting attention from the original boundary crossing.

The central trick is to turn a private decision into something that supposedly needs defending.

**When you defend a decision that was none of their business, you risk accepting the premise that they were entitled to question it.**

## The effect

Repeated intrusion can make someone feel watched, judged or guilty about ordinary activities. They may start changing their spending, habits or routines to avoid criticism, even when the decisions affect nobody else.

**Someone does not gain authority over your private life simply by passing judgement on it.** A decision that genuinely affects another person can be discussed on its merits. That is different from treating every personal choice as something requiring their approval.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
