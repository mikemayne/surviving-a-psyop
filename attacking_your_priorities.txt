# Attacking Your Priorities

## The Technique

**Example:** "You said the right things in the wrong order. That means your priorities are wrong."

This statement acknowledges that the content was correct, then treats the order in which it was said as proof of a wider fault in the speaker's values or judgement.

The key move is from **a potentially valid observation about presentation** to **an unsupported conclusion about character**. The criticism is no longer about what was said, but what the critic claims the order reveals.

## How It Works

The remark combines two elements:

1. **A concession:** "You said the right things." This apparently accepts the substance of the response.
2. **A sweeping inference:** "Your priorities are wrong." The speaker presents their interpretation of the sequence as a settled fact.

When used as a form of pressure, this can invite the target to explain their intentions, justify their values, or repeat the same points in a different order. If the standard is subjective or keeps changing, even a corrected response may attract another criticism.

## Possible Objective

In a manipulative interaction, the remark may be intended to:

- Undermine confidence even when the original statement was accurate.
- Shift attention away from the actual content towards a judgement about the person.
- Draw the person into defending their motives or moral priorities.
- Encourage excessive self-monitoring of wording, timing and emphasis.
- Establish an impossible standard in which there is always another way to find fault.

These are possible effects and motives, not conclusions that can be drawn from the sentence alone.

## Recognising the Pattern

Order and emphasis can matter. In some situations, they reveal what a person chose to foreground, especially when a consistent pattern appears across many decisions. Constructive feedback can explain why a particular order had practical consequences.

But **one ordering choice does not, by itself, establish someone's underlying priorities**. A stronger criticism would identify what the order actually changed or harmed, rather than assert a character flaw.

Be alert to a repeated pattern of acknowledging correct conduct while introducing new, vague or shifting grounds for condemnation.

## Further Considerations

Distinguish an actionable comment about clarity or urgency from a claim to know someone's hidden values. You can privately consider useful feedback without accepting an unsupported judgement about your character.

When such remarks are used repeatedly to provoke or unsettle you, there is no need to defend the order of every word.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
