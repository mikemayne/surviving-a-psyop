# Pretend Planning

**Effects:** Divert attention, Increase hypervigilance, Create uncertainty.

**Possible objectives:** Capture and control attention, Increase the effectiveness of other techniques.

## The technique

The technique uses a conversation just loud enough to attract the listener's attention, but not clear enough for them to make out the words.

The conversation sounds like planning, potentially something involving the victim.

The trick is that **they're not actually planning anything they want the victim to hear. They're trying to get the victim to listen.**

The immediate objective is to **grab the victim's attention and draw it away from whatever they're doing.** Once the victim starts concentrating on the conversation, the abusers can switch to other techniques, taking advantage of the attention they've captured.

Repeated over time, this can also encourage the victim to monitor background conversations and become increasingly distracted by things they cannot quite hear.

## How someone might describe it

- "I can hear them talking about me next door."
- "I can hear a conversation through the wall, but I can't quite make out what they're saying."
- "They sound like they're planning something."
- "I keep finding myself trying to listen to conversations I can't quite hear."
- "Whenever I try to concentrate on something, I get distracted by voices in the background."

**The distinctive feature is the attention trap:** indistinct speech draws the listener away from what they're doing and into trying to understand the conversation.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
