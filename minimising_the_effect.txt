# Minimising the Effect

**Effects:** Increase frustration, Increase self-doubt, Increase shame.

**Possible objectives:** Make serious conduct appear harmless, Shift attention onto the affected person's reaction, Discourage objections or reporting.

## The technique

Someone describes hurtful, threatening or repeated behaviour as harmless fun, then criticises the affected person for objecting to it.

For example:

- "We're just fucking with you. Why are you being such a dick about it?"
- "It was only a joke. Why are you making such a big deal out of it?"
- "We're just winding you up. You're the one who's taking it too seriously."
- "It's just banter. You're being unreasonable."

The wording invites an unfair comparison. The person responding may be reacting to a series of experiences, while the person doing the minimising presents only a supposedly trivial incident.

## How it works

The technique can combine several moves:

1. **Minimise the conduct.** Rebrand harmful or intimidating behaviour as teasing, banter or a joke.
2. **Erase the wider context.** Treat the latest incident in isolation, leaving out previous incidents and their cumulative effect.
3. **Make the reaction the problem.** Focus attention on the affected person's anger, distress or objection, rather than the conduct being challenged.
4. **Apply social pressure.** Imply that a reasonable person would laugh along, tolerate it or stop complaining.

The bigger the gap between the seriousness of the conduct and the harmless label applied to it, the more misleading the comparison may be.

Consider a hypothetical situation involving repeated credible threats or physical assaults. Calling that history "just fucking with you" would not make the conduct harmless. Nor would it establish that the person objecting was unreasonable.

## Why it matters

A single dismissive remark can make the person affected feel that they must justify their reaction before anyone will consider what happened. Repetition can add pressure to doubt their own judgement, stop speaking about events or accept unacceptable treatment.

The crucial distinction is between **what happened**, **how it was described**, and **how someone reacted**. A description such as "only joking" does not, by itself, settle the seriousness of the conduct.

This overlaps with [Downplaying trauma](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/downplaying_trauma) and [Victim blaming](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/victim_blaming). The particular feature here is the combination of **trivialising the behaviour** and **portraying the response as the real offence**.

Not every ill-judged joke is a deliberate manipulation attempt. Look at the conduct and context: whether someone acknowledges the impact, stops when asked, or instead repeatedly dismisses serious objections.

## Response

**Don't argue about whether it was "just a joke". Record what happened, including the wider context, if convenient. Disengage from provocation and use an appropriate reporting channel for threats or other serious conduct.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking help is not engaging with a provocation.
