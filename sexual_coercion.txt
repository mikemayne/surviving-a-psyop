### Sexual Coercion, Humiliation and Shame-Based Blackmail

**Effects:** Increase fear, Increase shame, Increase distress.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Induce compliance.

**Objective**

To humiliate an individual through a coerced sexual act and subsequently exploit their feelings of shame, embarrassment or fear of public exposure to intimidate, silence, isolate or control them. The threat of disclosure may be used repeatedly, or the information may actually be shared with others to cause further humiliation and reputational damage.

**Psychological Techniques**

This combines sexual coercion, degradation, shame conditioning, victim-blaming and sexual blackmail. The strategy exploits the social stigma associated with sexual behaviour, particularly behaviour that the individual would ordinarily find humiliating or distressing. It may also involve *DARVO* (Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender), in which responsibility is shifted from the perpetrator to the person subjected to coercion.

**Mechanism**

The individual is pressured or forced into performing a sexual act against their wishes. The perpetrator subsequently threatens to reveal the act to family, friends, colleagues or the wider public, potentially presenting it without the context of coercion.

This creates a second layer of abuse: the original sexual violation is followed by the threat of social humiliation. The perpetrator may attempt to make the individual feel complicit in their own abuse, suggesting that the coerced act reflects their character or sexual preferences rather than the circumstances in which it occurred.

Actual disclosure can extend the abuse by inviting others to participate in ridicule, ostracism or reputational attacks. The threat of disclosure can also be used to discourage reporting, seeking help or challenging the perpetrator.

**Countermeasure**

Recognise that coerced sexual behaviour does not establish consent, personal preference or moral culpability. The responsibility for sexual coercion and subsequent threats of exposure rests with the person responsible for those actions.

Attempts to exploit shame lose some of their power when the individual distinguishes what they were forced or pressured to do from what they freely chose to do. Trusted support, confidential professional advice and appropriate reporting channels can help counter isolation and intimidation.

**The shame belongs to the person who coerces and humiliates, not to the person subjected to the abuse.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
