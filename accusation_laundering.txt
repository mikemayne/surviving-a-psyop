# Accusation Laundering

**Effects:** Increase shame, Increase distress, Increase emotional reactivity.

**Possible objectives:** Discredit the individual, Influence third parties, Provoke a reaction.

## The technique

Someone makes an accusation. Later that day, they recount the accusation to a third party as if it were already an established fact.

That is the important change. The accusation has not become more reliable simply because it is being retold. The second conversation adds no independent evidence. It just drops the uncertainty.

For example, someone might first say, "I think Alex took my parcel." A few hours later, they tell someone else, "When Alex took my parcel..." The second statement treats the allegation as something that has already been established.

## How it works

This uses **presupposition**. Rather than asking whether the accusation is true, the speaker talks as though the answer is already known. A listener may accept the premise without stopping to question it.

There is also the **illusory truth effect**. Repeated claims can start to feel more familiar, and familiar statements can sometimes seem more credible. Repetition is not evidence, but people do not always keep those two things separate.

If the third party repeats the story, it can become even more misleading. Several people might appear to be confirming an accusation when, in fact, they all got it from the same original source. That is not independent corroboration.

If the person being accused happens to overhear the conversation, the remark may also provoke embarrassment, anger or a desire to defend themselves. Instead of questioning the original accusation, everyone can end up discussing the accused person's reaction.

## The important distinction

A person may genuinely believe an accusation and repeat it as fact without deliberately trying to manipulate anybody. The sequence on its own does not tell you why they said it.

But the effect on the listener can be the same: an allegation starts to sound like accepted knowledge when nothing has actually been established.

**An accusation doesn't become true because somebody starts talking about it as though it is.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
