# Projection

**Effects:** Confusion, self-doubt, defensiveness, shame and distraction.

**Possible objectives:** Shift attention away from someone's own conduct, put another person on the defensive, or influence how others interpret a disagreement.

## The technique

Projection is attributing your own feelings, intentions, behaviour or characteristics to somebody else.

In an argument, someone might accuse another person of being dishonest while concealing their own dishonesty, describe somebody else as aggressive while behaving aggressively themselves, or accuse another person of trying to control a situation when they are the one attempting to control it.

Projection is a recognised psychological process and is not always deliberate. People can do it without being aware of it. It can also be used deliberately as a form of manipulation. The fact that an accusation resembles the accuser's own behaviour does not, by itself, prove that projection is taking place or reveal their intentions.

## How it works

Projection can affect an interaction in several ways:

- **Accusatory projection:** Attributing your own behaviour or intentions to someone else. The accusation may be a reversal of what is actually happening.
- **Pre-emptive projection:** Raising an accusation first, potentially making it harder for the other person to identify or discuss similar behaviour by the accuser.
- **Reversal of responsibility:** Portraying the person responding to a problem as the person who caused it.
- **Creating defensiveness:** Making someone spend their time denying an accusation or explaining their character instead of discussing the conduct at issue.
- **Influencing other people's impressions:** Repeating a description of someone as dishonest, hostile or controlling can encourage others to interpret their actions through that description.

The common feature is a shift in attention. Instead of looking at what each person has actually said and done, the discussion becomes about an attributed motive or characteristic.

## The effect

Being repeatedly accused of things that do not fit your own intentions can be confusing and exhausting. You may feel compelled to defend yourself, provide increasingly detailed explanations or prove that you are not the person being described.

That can leave the original behaviour unexamined. It can also make an ordinary disagreement feel like a dispute about your entire character.

It is important not to reverse the same mistake. An accusation is not proof of the accuser's guilt, and noticing a possible resemblance is not enough to establish projection. Look at specific behaviour, context and evidence rather than trying to diagnose somebody else's psychology.

**You do not have to accept an accusation as an accurate description of you, and you do not have to prove the opposite in an endless argument.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/record_ignore_report) for the standard response.
