# Manufactured Agreement

**Effects:** Increase shame, Increase self-consciousness, Encourage self-censorship.

**Possible objectives:** Provoke a defensive reaction, Make ordinary entertainment feel like a moral test, Influence what someone watches or listens to.

## The technique

A song lyric or television programme says something controversial or offensive. Someone then accuses the listener or viewer of agreeing with it, even though they have never expressed that opinion.

For example:

- "You listen to that song, so you must agree with what it says."
- "You didn't turn the television off. That means you support it."
- "You laughed at that scene. So that's what you really think."
- "They apologised for making that song."

The accusation invents an opinion and assigns it to the person simply because they heard or watched something.

The last example is a subtler variation. By invoking the artist's supposed apology, it suggests that even the creator now considers the song objectionable. That can invite the listener to feel ashamed of enjoying it, without actually showing what the listener believes. Whether an apology happened is a separate factual question; either way, it does not establish agreement with the lyrics.

## How it works

This is **guilt by association** and **false attribution of beliefs**. It replaces what someone actually thinks with a claim about what they supposedly endorse.

People listen to music for the melody, watch fiction for entertainment, encounter offensive material by chance and sometimes laugh at jokes they do not agree with. None of those things establishes their personal beliefs.

If the accusation is used as pressure, it can tempt someone to defend a position they never took. It can also make ordinary entertainment feel like a test, encouraging them to monitor their reactions or avoid material they would otherwise enjoy.

**The important distinction:** Hearing, watching or enjoying something is not the same as endorsing everything said in it. An actual accusation needs to be distinguished from an unrelated comment about the media itself.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.
