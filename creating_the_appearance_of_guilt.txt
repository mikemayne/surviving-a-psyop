# Creating the Appearance of Guilt

**Effects:** Increase vigilance, Increase self-consciousness, Undermine confidence.

**Possible objectives:** Discredit the individual, Increase the effectiveness of other techniques.

Many of the techniques described in this guide could share an immediate objective: to make the victim behave in a suspicious, paranoid or hesitant manner in public.

Repeated accusations, confusing statements, subtle provocations and psychological pressure can make someone increasingly conscious of their surroundings. They may start looking around, watching other people, hesitating before speaking or reacting unusually to ordinary conversations.

To an outside observer who doesn't understand what the person has been experiencing, this behaviour might appear suspicious.

If someone is already being accused of wrongdoing, their reactions could then be presented as further evidence of guilt. Other people may become suspicious of them, avoid them or treat them differently.

This creates a potentially self-reinforcing cycle: the more uncomfortable someone becomes in public, the more their behaviour may be misinterpreted, increasing their discomfort.

**Defence:** Don't engage with provocations or try to prove your innocence to strangers. Continue with ordinary activities wherever possible. Remember that appearing nervous, distracted or uncomfortable is not evidence of wrongdoing.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
