# Staged Compliments

**Effects:** Increase receptiveness, gratitude, trust and compliance pressure.

**Possible objectives:** Capture someone's attention and make them more receptive to what follows.

## The technique

Someone offers a compliment immediately before introducing a suggestion, request or claim they want you to accept. The praise might concern your intelligence, judgement, experience or ability to see things clearly.

The compliment makes the listener feel recognised and gives them a reason to pay attention. It can also create a subtle expectation of warmth or reciprocity, making it harder to dismiss what follows.

The compliment is the hook. The intended influence comes afterwards.

## How it works

- **Positive attention:** Praise makes the interaction feel rewarding and encourages the listener to keep listening.
- **Reciprocity:** A person who has just been made to feel good about themselves may feel an inclination to respond warmly in return.
- **Borrowed credibility:** The compliment can make the speaker seem perceptive or personally supportive, lending the following message an undeserved sense of credibility.
- **Timed transition:** Moving directly from praise to a request or persuasive claim can make the two feel like one continuous friendly interaction.

A compliment does not have to be extravagant. A brief observation that someone is unusually perceptive, sensible or trustworthy can be enough to secure their attention.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
