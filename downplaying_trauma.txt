# Downplaying trauma

**Effects:** Increase distress, Increase shame, Increase self-doubt.

**Possible objectives:** Minimise the impact of distressing experiences, undermine confidence in the individual's response, discourage discussion or help-seeking.

## The technique

Downplaying trauma means treating a distressing experience as insignificant, harmless or unworthy of the reaction it has produced.

Someone may suggest that an experience was not serious, that other people have been through worse, or that the individual should simply get over it. They may describe an abnormal or frightening experience as ordinary, or imply that continuing to be affected by it is a sign of weakness.

The experience itself does not need to be denied. The focus is shifted onto the person's reaction, making that reaction appear unreasonable or excessive.

## How it works

There are several ways to downplay trauma:

- **Minimisation:** Describing an experience as trivial or suggesting it should not have had a lasting effect.
- **Comparative suffering:** Pointing out that other people have experienced worse things, as though this makes the individual's own experience unimportant.
- **Questioning resilience:** Suggesting that a reasonable or strong person would have shrugged it off.
- **Focusing on intent:** Arguing that an experience should not matter because nobody supposedly intended to cause harm.
- **Normalisation:** Presenting distressing or abnormal experiences as ordinary events that everyone encounters.
- **Pressure to move on:** Suggesting that the individual should have forgotten about the experience or put it behind them, regardless of its continuing effects.

These responses move attention away from what happened and towards whether the individual is entitled to feel affected by it.

## The effect

Repeatedly having distress dismissed can make someone question their own reactions. They may start minimising their own suffering, avoid discussing what happened or feel that they have no legitimate reason to seek support.

It can also make it harder to explain an experience clearly. The individual may spend more time defending the fact that they were affected than describing what happened in the first place.

**A person's distress does not become insignificant because somebody else tells them it should be.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/record_ignore_report) for the standard response.
