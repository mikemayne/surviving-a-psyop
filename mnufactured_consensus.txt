MANUFACTURED CONSENSUS
--------------------------

THE TECHNIQUE

Manufactured consensus is the deliberate presentation of an accusation or
judgement as though it were shared by a large number of independent people.
A person may claim "everyone agrees," recruit others to repeat a prepared
statement, or speak for a group without permission. The aim is not
necessarily to prove anything. It is to give a claim the appearance of
social authority and to make the target feel alone in resisting it.

WHY IT WORKS

Humans use the judgements of other people as a shortcut when information is
incomplete. This is usually sensible: a warning confirmed by several
genuinely independent sources deserves more attention than a lone opinion.
Manufactured consensus exploits that shortcut. Repetition is made to look
like independent confirmation, while the origin of the claim is concealed.
It can also create a fear of social exclusion: even someone who knows an
accusation is inaccurate may worry that friends, colleagues or strangers
have accepted it.

RECOGNISING THE PATTERN

The key question is whether the supposed agreement can actually be traced to
independent sources. "People are saying" may refer to no more than one
speaker. Several people repeating identical wording may be repeating a
rumour rather than reporting personal knowledge. Equally, overheard remarks
or similar comments from unrelated people do not by themselves establish
coordination. Distinguish direct statements addressed to you, statements you
can verify, and impressions about what others believe.

PRACTICAL RESPONSE

Do not conduct an opinion poll among strangers or attempt to challenge every
rumour. Ask for a specific source, fact or written statement if one is
relevant to a real decision. In workplaces or institutions, request that any
allegation affecting you be put in clear terms with an opportunity to
respond. Continue judging yourself by your conduct and reliable
relationships, not by vague claims about a crowd. Where a demonstrably false
statement causes concrete harm, document that harm and seek proportionate
advice.
