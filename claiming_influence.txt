# Claiming Influence Over Others

**Effects:** Increase fear, Undermine confidence, Increase distress.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Induce compliance.

One intimidation technique is to claim influence over everyone in your life, including friends, family, colleagues and complete strangers.

Examples include:

- "He did that because I told him to."
- "I'll tell them you said that."
- "They only did that because we asked them to."
- "Everyone you know is working with us."

The trick is to take credit for ordinary events that would probably have happened anyway. By claiming responsibility after the fact, someone can create the impression that they control far more than they actually do.

Threatening to pass on your opinions works similarly. If you complain about a supermarket pizza, someone might claim they'll tell the supermarket what you said. That doesn't mean they have any influence over the supermarket, or that they'll actually contact anyone.

**Don't assume that someone claiming influence actually possesses it.** Consider whether an event would have happened anyway. Don't allow unsupported claims to undermine your trust in other people or make you afraid to express ordinary opinions.

Someone claiming to control your world is not the same as someone controlling it.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
