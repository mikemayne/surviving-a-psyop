# Accusations of False Reports

**Example:** "He's just made a false report against X!"

### How it works

Accusing someone of making false reports can be used to discourage them from contacting the police, making complaints or seeking help from authorities.

The accusation may be made loudly or repeatedly, potentially creating anxiety about being investigated or prosecuted, damaging the person's credibility, or making them reluctant to report further incidents.

It can also exploit confusion between a report that cannot be substantiated and one that was deliberately fabricated.

### How to respond

**An honest report that turns out to be mistaken is not automatically a false report.**

Report what you directly observed, distinguish facts from interpretations, and avoid speculation presented as certainty.

Keep copies of your reports and any responses you receive. Don't engage with provocative accusations. Let the appropriate authorities assess the information.

You have a right to raise genuine concerns in good faith, even when you cannot prove what happened.
