# How to collaborate

This is an open project. If you've experienced an upsetting, confusing or manipulative interaction, you may have a useful perspective to contribute. You don't have to know why something happened, or who was responsible, to describe what you observed and what helped you respond.

You can write your own version of this guide, suggest a correction, or offer a new section. **Sharing is entirely optional.** There is no obligation to disclose personal experiences.

**No technical knowledge required.** You can write, revise and submit a contribution by talking to ChatGPT in ordinary language. It will explain any account setup or button-clicking when needed. You do not need to learn Git, coding or pull requests.

**Getting support:** If you feel unwell, please consider contacting your GP or mental health services. If you feel unsafe, contact the police (or call 999 if you are in immediate danger). You can seek help alongside writing or contributing here; there's no need to wait until you've finished.

**Submission formats — plain text only:** I will only accept `.txt` files containing ordinary plain text. **No PDFs, images, audio, video, Word documents, Markdown (`.md`) files, HTML, JavaScript, scripts, executable files, attachments or other formats.** Ask ChatGPT to prepare a simple `.txt` file without embedded media or code. Submissions containing other file types will not be accepted.

**Privacy before contributing:** Anything submitted through GitHub to this book is **publicly visible**, including your contribution, GitHub username, submission discussion and commit history. If you want to avoid associating it with your real identity, consider a separate pseudonymous GitHub account and email address, keep your email private in GitHub settings, and check the author details attached to commits. These precautions reduce exposure but cannot guarantee anonymity. You can draft privately and choose not to submit.

## 1. Start with a more private conversation

You can use [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) to organise your thoughts and draft an account. Start a new **Temporary Chat** rather than a normal saved conversation. Temporary chats stay out of your chat history and aren't used to improve OpenAI's models while temporary, although a copy may be kept for up to 30 days for safety purposes. Temporary Chat is **not** an anonymous or confidential reporting service.

If you want ChatGPT to work directly with GitHub in the same conversation, select **Personalized** when starting Temporary Chat; **Unpersonalized** does not use connected plugins. If you would rather not connect an account, you can simply ask ChatGPT to produce text and upload it yourself.

Even in Temporary Chat, avoid entering names or sensitive details that aren't necessary. You can replace them with neutral descriptions before telling your story.

## 2. Set up GitHub only if you decide to submit

**You can skip this section while drafting.** If you later decide to contribute your work, ask ChatGPT to guide you through each step. It can explain unfamiliar terms and tell you exactly where to click.

You will need a free account at [github.com](https://github.com/) if you don't have one. Consider creating a separate account with a pseudonymous username and an email address that does not include your real name or identify you. In GitHub's email settings, keep your email address private and consider its GitHub-provided no-reply address for commits. Check the name and email that will appear in any commit before approving it. If ChatGPT cannot create the account or fork the project for you, it can walk you through those few clicks on [the source repository](https://github.com/mikemayne/surviving-a-psyop).

In ChatGPT, go to **Settings > Plugins**, find GitHub, connect your account if the plugin is available to you, and review the permissions you are granting. Ask ChatGPT to work on **your fork** (your copy), not the original repository. When connected and authorised, ChatGPT can help write files and submit your contribution for review. You do not need to learn Git commands.

**Important:** This is a public repository. Your public fork, commit history, GitHub username and any pull request can be viewed by other people. A pseudonym or private email setting does not guarantee anonymity. You can do all the writing without forking or publishing anything.

## 3. Describe your experience in your own words

Tell ChatGPT what you directly saw, heard or experienced; enough context to make the event understandable; how it affected you; and what you did in response. Distinguish what you observed from any interpretation or suspicion about its cause. It's fine to be uncertain.

Then ask ChatGPT to turn the experience into a **general, self-contained section in the style of this guide**: explain a possible pattern of manipulation or pressure, give a short illustrative example, and suggest calm, practical responses. The aim is to describe a recognisable situation, not to make accusations about particular people.

The **Contribute with ChatGPT** link at the top uses this prompt. You can also copy and paste it into a Temporary Chat yourself:

> I want to contribute to the open book at https://github.com/mikemayne/surviving-a-psyop. Please guide me through EVERYTHING in ordinary language. Assume I know nothing about Git, GitHub, code, forks, branches or pull requests. Ask only one simple question at a time. First help me write or revise a short chapter in the style of the existing book. Ask what I would like to contribute. Don't invent facts or motives; distinguish observations from interpretations and consider simpler explanations. Remove names and details that could identify me or other people. Let me review and edit the draft until I am happy. Do not ask me to create accounts yet. Only if I say I want to submit it, explain that a GitHub account, public fork, commits and submission may expose identifying details. Then help me connect GitHub to ChatGPT if available and handle as much as possible for me: fork the book, create the text file and submit it to the original author for review. If you cannot perform a step yourself, tell me exactly what button to click, one step at a time, without jargon. Show me exactly what will be made public and ask my explicit approval before uploading or submitting anything. If I do not wish to publish, help me keep the draft private. Begin by asking what I would like to contribute.

## 4. Remove identifying information — then check again

Ask ChatGPT to review the draft specifically for information that might identify **you or someone else**. Check names, usernames, workplaces, schools, exact dates and places, distinctive personal relationships, unusual wording, links, photographs, documents, and details that could identify someone when combined.

Don't assume that replacing names is enough: a distinctive incident can reveal identities even without names. Review the final text yourself, and consider asking a trusted person to check it independently. AI can miss clues, so complete anonymity cannot be guaranteed. Don't upload raw evidence, private records or identifiable material to a public repository.

Keep the description fair: present events as observations or personal recollections, not established facts about another person's motives, and avoid naming or accusing identifiable individuals. If you have material relevant to an official investigation, consider appropriate professional advice before publishing it.

## 5. Decide whether and how to share

If you're comfortable publishing, simply tell ChatGPT: **"I'm happy with the draft. Please help me submit it to the book for review."** ChatGPT can guide you through creating a new `.txt` file in your GitHub copy and proposing it to the original author. It can explain each step, carry out supported actions with your permission, or tell you exactly which button to click. **Inspect the exact file and commit before approving the upload.**

You then have several choices:

- **Suggest it for this guide:** ask ChatGPT to **submit the draft to the author for review**. Behind the scenes, GitHub calls this a *pull request*. It is public and may be reviewed, discussed, edited or declined; you do not need to understand the technical process.
- **Publish your own version:** keep your fork and share its link if you wish. You can adapt the guide to your own priorities.
- **Keep it to yourself:** save the draft locally, share it privately with someone you trust, or do nothing. You don't need to publish to make the writing worthwhile.

Check the final publication view, filename, Git commit author details, and any discussion text for identifying information. Changes can remain visible in Git history and external archives even if you later delete them.

Contributions offered for inclusion in this repository should be available under its [CC0 1.0 Universal dedication](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).

**The central principle:** retain control over your account. Use AI to help you write clearly, protect other people's privacy as carefully as your own, and share only what you freely choose to share.
