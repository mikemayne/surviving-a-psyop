# Logic Traps

A logic trap is an argument, question or statement that encourages you to accept a false premise and then reason from it towards a predetermined conclusion.

The objective is often to make you defend yourself against an accusation, justify an ordinary decision, or choose between options that somebody else has defined for you.

**The simplest defence is to recognise the false premise and refuse to accept it.**

## False Dilemmas

"You can run, but you can't hide."

This statement presents two options: running and hiding. Both imply that you are being pursued and have something to fear.

But these are not your only options. You don't have to run or hide. You can carry on with your life and do whatever you ordinarily choose to do.

Other examples include:

- "Either you cooperate or you're against us."
- "If you don't explain yourself, you must have something to hide."
- "You can admit what you've done now or face the consequences later."

The trick is to convince you that the choices presented are the only choices available.

**You are not obliged to choose between two options simply because somebody presents them to you.**

## Loaded Questions

"Why are you trying to conceal your guilt?"

Answering this question directly requires you to accept that you are guilty and attempting to conceal it.

Other examples include:

- "When did you stop lying?"
- "Why are you so frightened of being investigated?"
- "Why do you keep behaving suspiciously?"
- "What are you trying to hide?"

These questions contain assumptions that have not been established.

You do not need to answer a loaded question on its own terms. You can reject the assumption, ask for clarification, or simply disengage.

## Guilt by Interpretation

"Doing that makes you look guilty."

This is particularly effective because almost any behaviour can be interpreted negatively if somebody is determined to do so.

For example:

- Leaving a situation means you're running away.
- Staying means you're trying to appear innocent.
- Remaining silent means you're hiding something.
- Explaining yourself means you're making excuses.
- Contacting a solicitor means you're worried about prosecution.
- Refusing to engage means you're being evasive.

Notice that every possible response can be given a negative interpretation.

**When somebody can interpret every outcome as evidence of the same conclusion, their interpretation tells you very little about whether that conclusion is true.**

You cannot control how other people choose to interpret your behaviour. You can control your own decisions.

Make those decisions according to your interests, obligations and rights, rather than somebody else's invented rules.

## Circular Reasoning

"We know you're guilty because you're behaving suspiciously. Your behaviour is suspicious because we know you're guilty."

Circular reasoning uses a conclusion to justify itself without supplying independent evidence.

Another example:

"Only a guilty person would deny this. You're denying it, therefore you're guilty."

The argument assumes precisely what it claims to establish.

There is no need to spend time disproving an argument that contains no independent justification for its conclusion.

## Double Binds

A double bind is a situation in which contradictory demands make every available response appear wrong.

For example:

- "You must defend yourself, but defending yourself proves you're guilty."
- "You must trust us, but asking how you can trust us demonstrates that you're untrustworthy."
- "You must behave naturally, but we're going to scrutinise everything you do."

These arrangements can create considerable stress because they encourage you to search for a correct response where none has been permitted.

The solution is not to find the perfect response. It is to recognise that the conditions are unreasonable and stop allowing them to govern your decisions.

## Appeals to Imagined Authority

"Any police officer would consider that suspicious."

"That's how the courts will interpret your behaviour."

"Your solicitor would tell you the same thing."

These statements attempt to borrow authority from institutions or professionals who may not have expressed any such opinion.

Someone threatening or intimidating you is not a reliable source of legal advice.

If you need legal guidance, obtain it independently from a qualified professional. Do not change your behaviour merely because somebody claims to know how the police, courts or other authorities will interpret it.

## Further considerations
You do not need to identify every logical fallacy or win an argument with somebody using these techniques.

A few simple principles are sufficient:

1. **Question the premise.** Does the argument rely on something that hasn't been established?
2. **Reject false choices.** Are there other reasonable options?
3. **Avoid unnecessary self-defence.** You do not have to answer every accusation or hypothetical question.
4. **Make your own decisions.** Act according to your interests, values and legal obligations.
5. **Disengage when appropriate.** You are not obliged to continue an unproductive or intimidating conversation.

In formal situations, legitimate questions and legal obligations may require a response. Seek independent advice rather than treating every difficult question as manipulation.

## The Important Principle

A logic trap only works as an argument if you accept the assumptions on which it depends.

You can recognise what somebody is trying to make you think without accepting their interpretation, adopting their rules or changing your behaviour.

You don't have to prove that you're innocent by acting innocent according to somebody else's definition.

**You don't have to solve the trap. You can reject its premise and carry on with your life.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
