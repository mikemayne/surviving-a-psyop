# Attention Capture and Conditioning

**Effects:** Divert attention, Increase vigilance, Increase distraction.

**Possible objectives:** Make particular stimuli hard to ignore, Interrupt ordinary activities, Increase the influence of later messages.

## The principle

Repeatedly draw attention to a cue and make it feel significant. Over time, the person may start noticing or anticipating the cue without needing another direct request.

The immediate goal need not be agreement with a particular claim. It can simply be to occupy attention.

## How it works

A repeated phrase, abrupt interruption or conspicuous compliment catches someone's attention. At unpredictable intervals, it is followed by criticism, reassurance, a threat or another emotionally charged message.

The combination can make the cue seem important. Someone may begin listening for it, checking its meaning or interrupting what they were doing whenever it appears.

Intermittent reinforcement can strengthen learned attention, although repetition can also lead to habituation.

**The defining structure is cue, attention, reinforcement and a possible learned expectation.**

An analyser should look for that sequence in the description, not assume that noticing a repeated cue establishes an external campaign. The principle can operate in ordinary advertising, coercive relationships and other settings.

This meta-pattern relates to Staged Compliments, Pretend Planning, Good Cop/Bad Cop, False Endings and Cumulative Psychological Pressure.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**
