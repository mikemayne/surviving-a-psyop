# Loaded Moral Accusations — “HOW Racist Are You?”

**Effects:** Increase shame, Undermine confidence, Increase emotional reactivity.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Discredit the individual.

This technique involves accusing someone of saying or doing something offensive, then loudly demanding, “HOW racist are you?”, “HOW homophobic are you?”, or a similar question implying serious moral wrongdoing.

The trick is that the question **presupposes the accusation is true**. Rather than asking whether the individual actually said or did anything wrong, it asks them to account for how morally reprehensible they supposedly are.

This might be combined with one of the more advanced techniques to attempt to make you say you said something, or associate a bodily function with something.

The exaggerated outrage can serve several psychological purposes:

- **Public humiliation:** Drawing attention from bystanders who may hear the accusation without knowing what actually happened.
- **Presupposition:** Presenting an unproven accusation as an established fact.
- **Provocation:** Encouraging the accused to respond angrily or defensively, potentially making them appear unreasonable.
- **Reputational damage:** Encouraging others to associate the individual with a socially unacceptable attitude or behaviour.
- **Defensive entrapment:** Forcing the individual into defending their character rather than establishing whether the original accusation has any basis.

The technique is particularly effective when the original accusation is vague, exaggerated or entirely fabricated. The target may feel compelled to demonstrate that they are not prejudiced, inadvertently accepting the accuser's framing of the interaction.

**How to respond:** Ignore the accusation completely. Even asking what the speaker meant or correcting a misrepresentation draws you into a discussion. Do not argue, explain yourself or say anything in response. Carry on with your day. If a credible threat or a genuine formal allegation arises, deal with it independently through the police or an appropriate official process, not with the person making provocative remarks.

A person's indignation, however loudly expressed, is not evidence that their accusation is true.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
