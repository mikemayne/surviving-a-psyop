# Attributing Displaced Anger

**Effects:** Confusion, defensiveness, resentment, misplaced guilt.

**Possible objectives:** Shift responsibility, influence how someone interprets a disagreement, redirect hostility towards a third person.

## The technique

A statement such as "He's taking it all out on X" gives an explanation for someone's behaviour by claiming that their anger is really about something or someone else.

It may be used to suggest that one person is behaving badly towards another because of frustration caused elsewhere. The explanation can sound sympathetic or reasonable, but it also assigns motives, causes and roles in the situation.

The statement might be true. People sometimes do displace frustration onto others. The technique is in presenting that explanation as established fact when it has not been established.

## How it works

The statement can influence the way an interaction is understood:

- **Assigning a hidden cause:** The speaker tells the listener what supposedly explains someone's behaviour, rather than sticking to what was actually said or done.
- **Redirecting responsibility:** Attention moves away from the person's own choices and towards the person or event said to have caused their frustration.
- **Creating a third-party conflict:** The listener may be encouraged to resent the supposed cause, sympathise with one person, or take sides in a disagreement.
- **Reframing behaviour:** Conduct that would otherwise be judged on its own merits is presented as an understandable reaction to something else.
- **Creating misplaced guilt:** Someone may feel responsible for another person's behaviour because they have been told that the person is taking their frustration out on somebody else.

The statement can therefore do more than describe an interaction. It supplies a ready-made explanation and invites the listener to adopt it.

## The effect

Once a motive has been supplied, it can be difficult to separate the actual behaviour from the story about why it happened. The listener may spend time working out who is really to blame, feel responsible for keeping the peace, or become hostile towards a third person.

You do not need to decide what somebody secretly feels or why they acted as they did. Look at the behaviour itself. If someone is treating another person badly, that behaviour can be addressed without accepting an unsupported explanation for it.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/record_ignore_report) for the standard response.
