# Invoking an Imagined Audience

**Effects:** Increase social pressure, Increase fear, Undermine confidence.

**Possible objectives:** Intimidate the individual, Influence behaviour, Discourage action.

## The technique

The speaker invokes the anticipated reaction of an absent person, institution or audience as a way of influencing the individual.

Examples include:

- "The press are going to hate that."
- "The jury won't like that."
- "The police are going to love this."
- "The authorities won't be happy."
- "Everyone is going to hear about this."

The audience might be the press, a jury, the police, an employer, family members or the general public. The speaker presents its reaction as though it is already known, predictable or inevitable.

## How it works

The statement shifts attention away from what is actually being discussed and towards what an imagined audience supposedly thinks or will do.

It can make the individual feel watched, judged or outnumbered. It can also imply that powerful people are already aligned against them, without anyone from those groups having expressed that view.

The imagined audience lends the speaker's opinion borrowed authority. Instead of explaining why an action is wrong or addressing the evidence, the speaker suggests that some larger group will condemn it.

The technique can also be used to discourage someone from taking an ordinary action. The individual is invited to anticipate embarrassment, punishment, rejection or public hostility before anything has happened.

## Variations

The audience does not have to be a formal institution. It can be a vague collective such as "everyone", "people" or "the whole town".

The predicted reaction can be positive or negative. "The police are going to love this" can be just as intimidating as "the police are going to hate this". Both statements use an absent audience to create pressure.

## The central trick

A prediction about how other people will react is not evidence that they have reached that conclusion. The speaker is asking you to respond to an imagined judgement rather than the actual issue.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
