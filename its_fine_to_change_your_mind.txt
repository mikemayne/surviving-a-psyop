### It's absolutely fine to change your mind

It's natural to want to be consistent. Most people don't like changing their minds, especially after they've already agreed to something or made a commitment.

Psychologists call this a *preference for consistency*. We want our actions to match our beliefs, and we want other people to see us as consistent.

But consistency isn't always a good thing.

If you have agreed to something under pressure, intimidation or duress, it's perfectly reasonable to reconsider that agreement once you're no longer under pressure.

You don't have to defend a decision simply because you made it. You don't have to justify something you said when you were frightened, distressed or trying to get out of a difficult situation.

Changing your mind doesn't make you weak, dishonest or inconsistent. It can simply mean that you're now making a decision freely, rather than responding to pressure.

**You are allowed to change your mind. You don't owe anyone consistency at the expense of your own judgement.  You don't owe an abuser anything at all.**
