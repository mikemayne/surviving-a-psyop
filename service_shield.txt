### Service-Based Moral Immunity

This technique involves claiming that a person's past or present service to their country, community, employer, or some supposedly greater cause places them beyond ordinary moral scrutiny or criticism.

The individual may invoke military service, public service, national security, personal sacrifice, or the importance of their work to imply that questioning their conduct is inherently disrespectful, unpatriotic, or morally wrong.

The psychological objective is to establish a hierarchy of moral worth in which the individual's claimed contributions outweigh any harm they cause. Criticism is reframed as an attack on the institution they represent, the people they supposedly protect, or the sacrifices they have made, rather than an examination of their actual behaviour.

This can also exploit feelings of gratitude, patriotism, guilt, and social obligation to discourage complaints or demands for accountability.

**The central fallacy is simple: doing good things does not confer permission to do bad things.** Service may deserve recognition and gratitude, but it does not provide immunity from criticism, ethical obligations, or the law.

Indeed, people entrusted with public authority or responsibility should generally be held to higher standards of accountability, not lower ones.
