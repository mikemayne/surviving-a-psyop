# Threatening Consequences for Seeking Help

**Effects:** Increase fear, Increase distress, Undermine confidence.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Discourage reporting.

## The Technique

This tactic tries to stop someone from obtaining assistance by making the act of asking for help appear dangerous. The threat may concern losing a job, damaging a relationship, being punished by an institution, attracting unwanted attention, or provoking further mistreatment. Sometimes the speaker frames reporting as betrayal: "If you complain, you'll only make things worse for everyone." The aim is to close the routes that could interrupt the situation.

## Why It Works

Seeking help already requires effort and trust. Threats add a cost to that decision and can make the person doubt whether authorities, friends or advisers will respond fairly. The effect is particularly strong when the threatening person holds power over employment, accommodation, care or daily life. A person may postpone action indefinitely, waiting for a supposedly safer moment that never arrives.

## Recognising the Pattern

A sincere warning about practical consequences is different from intimidation. A competent adviser might honestly explain that a complaint process can be stressful or that information may need to be disclosed. A coercive warning is designed to prevent the individual from making an informed choice, often without offering a credible route to protection or advice. Pay attention to concrete threats, not just someone's pessimistic opinion about whether a report will succeed.

## Further considerations
Make important decisions after consulting a source independent of the person applying pressure. Find out what confidentiality, whistleblowing, advocacy or safeguarding arrangements actually exist. Preserve clear evidence of any explicit retaliatory threat. Use the appropriate channel and share only information needed for the concern being raised. If there is an immediate safety risk, prioritise immediate protection. No one needs to resolve the entire underlying dispute before asking a professional what their options are.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
