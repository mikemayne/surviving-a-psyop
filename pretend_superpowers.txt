# Pretend Superpowers

**Effects:** Increase confusion, Increase suggestibility, Undermine confidence.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Create false beliefs, Provoke a reaction.

## The technique

Make someone believe they possess extraordinary abilities by repeatedly engineering apparent demonstrations of those abilities.

One example would be making someone believe they have an extraordinarily accurate sense of time.

Imagine someone estimates that the time is 8:08. They pick up their phone, which displays 8:07, and as they look at it the clock changes to 8:08.

Nothing appears unusual about the clock. It simply advances by one minute, exactly as a clock normally would.

Now imagine the same thing happening repeatedly. The person starts to believe they have an almost supernatural ability to predict the time.

In this hypothetical scenario, the displayed time is being manipulated. The person's actual ability to estimate time has not changed.

## Reinforcing the illusion

Subconscious priming could potentially influence which time the person expects to see. Manipulated feedback could then appear to confirm their prediction.

Social reinforcement could strengthen the effect. Another person loudly expressing amazement at each apparent success makes the experience seem even more extraordinary.

Repeated apparent successes build confidence. The individual may begin to regard their exceptional ability as an established fact, particularly when another person appears to independently confirm it.

The important psychological mechanisms are **manipulated feedback, reinforcement, expectation and social validation**.

The effect need not be limited to predicting the time. The general principle is to make an ordinary or engineered coincidence appear to demonstrate an extraordinary personal ability.

## The cumulative effect

A single apparent coincidence might be dismissed. Repeated apparent coincidences, particularly when reinforced by another person's reactions, can become much more persuasive.

The individual may begin looking for further demonstrations of their supposed ability, paying more attention to apparent successes and less attention to failures.

The objective is to make the person trust an interpretation that is not supported by independent evidence.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
