# Weaponised Literalism — Arguing About Words Instead of Meaning

### The technique

An argument can be diverted by interpreting ordinary words according to an excessively narrow or literal definition, rather than the meaning that is clear from the context.

Instead of engaging with what someone is saying, the other person challenges an approximation, figure of speech or minor choice of words. A straightforward conversation can then become a lengthy dispute about terminology.

### What it can look like

- "You said *everyone* was upset, but you haven't spoken to literally everyone."
- "You said it happens *constantly*, but it didn't happen yesterday afternoon."
- "You said I ignored your question, but I replied to you." The reply may not have answered the question.
- "You said you contacted me *repeatedly*. Three messages don't count as repeatedly."

The objection might be technically arguable while having little bearing on the point being made.

### Why it works

Repeated demands for precision can shift the discussion away from the underlying issue and onto the speaker's wording. They may feel obliged to defend casual expressions, explain obvious context and keep revising their statements.

When used deliberately, this can exhaust the speaker or create the misleading impression that their substantive point has been disproved, when only a minor phrasing issue has been identified.

### Further considerations
Clarify your intended meaning once, in plain language. For example: "By everyone, I meant the people I spoke to."

Then return to the original point or question. You can acknowledge a genuine correction without allowing it to replace the subject of the conversation.

Where exact language matters, such as in a formal report, make the wording more precise. Otherwise, you do not have to keep defending harmless shorthand. If the discussion repeatedly goes nowhere, disengage and consider addressing the substantive issue in writing.

### An important distinction

Not every request for clarification is manipulation. People can reasonably disagree about definitions, and technical, medical and legal questions sometimes require very precise wording.

The more useful distinction is between **trying to understand what someone means** and **repeatedly arguing about minor wording after that meaning is already clear**.

**Clarify once. Return to the point. Don't get trapped in an endless argument over words.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
