# Induced Reactions as Evidence

**Effects:** Increase defensiveness, Increase shame, Undermine credibility.

**Possible objectives:** Provoke a reaction, Discredit an individual, Shift attention away from the original conduct.

## The principle

Create pressure that may produce a reaction, then present that reaction as evidence of a separate accusation.

The response becomes the story. The circumstances that preceded it are left out.

## How it works

Someone repeatedly makes insulting remarks. The recipient eventually becomes angry. The anger is then presented as proof that the recipient is aggressive, with no mention of the repeated provocation.

The same structure can exploit hesitation, embarrassment, walking away, seeking reassurance or making an exaggerated claim under pressure.

**A reaction can be real without the proposed explanation for it being true.**

To recognise this meta-pattern, identify the preceding pressure, the observable reaction and the conclusion drawn from it. Ask whether the conclusion actually follows from the reaction. Anger does not prove an aggressive character; leaving does not prove guilt.

This mechanism appears in Creating the Appearance of Guilt, The Guilty Always Run, Accusation Baiting and Claim Inflation.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**
