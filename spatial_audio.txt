# Spatial Audio — Voices From Unexpected Directions

**Effects:** Confusion, fear, uncertainty about the source of a voice, exaggerated perceptions of surveillance capabilities.

**Possible objectives:** Make someone believe a voice is coming from somewhere it isn't. Convince them that technology is being used which is considerably more sophisticated than the equipment actually required.

## How it works

Head-Related Transfer Functions (HRTFs) describe how the shape of your head, ears and body affects sounds arriving from different directions.

By processing recorded speech using an HRTF, it is possible to make a voice appear to originate from a different direction from the physical speakers reproducing it, including above or behind the listener.

A car is a suitable environment for attempting this because the driver's head occupies a relatively predictable position. Knowledge of the listener's head and ear geometry can improve the spatial effect.

A car with ordinary front and rear speakers can reproduce spatially processed audio. The speakers do not need to be physically modified.

## Using a connected phone

A phone connected to a car stereo can provide the audio signal. If the phone is compromised, software on it could generate or play a voice that has already been processed using an HRTF.

The car's existing speakers then reproduce that signal.

This does not require specialist speakers or an additional device installed inside the car. The spatial processing can take place on the phone.

The accuracy of the effect depends on the speaker arrangement, the processing and the acoustics of the vehicle.

## Exaggerating technological capabilities

A voice apparently coming from above someone's head could be used to suggest that it was produced by an extraordinary surveillance or neurological technology.

The content of the voice could reinforce that impression by making claims about the technology supposedly being used.

The important point is that **the apparent direction of a voice can be manipulated using ordinary audio technology.** A voice that appears to come from above the listener does not require a speaker above their head.
