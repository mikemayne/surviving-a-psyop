# Outcome Inflation and Deflation

**Effects:** Distort expectations, encourage overconfidence or discouragement.

**Possible objectives:** Influence decisions.

## The Pattern

Predictions about the consequences of someone's actions can be exaggerated in either direction. One person might say, "He's going to be a millionaire", while another dismisses the same work with "Nobody will use that."

Both statements make sweeping forecasts without explaining the evidence behind them. If used deliberately to influence someone, the first can inflate expectations while the second can undermine confidence or motivation.

These predictions do not have to be addressed directly to the person concerned. But an overheard remark, particularly one without clear context, may not be about them at all.

## Why It Works

People decide what to pursue partly by imagining the likely rewards and disappointments. An emphatic prediction can become an anchor for those expectations even when it is little more than an opinion.

Exaggerated optimism may encourage overcommitment, unrealistic financial decisions or a sense that anything short of extraordinary success is failure. A project that produces a modest but worthwhile result can feel disappointing when it has been measured against a fantasy.

Exaggerated pessimism may encourage someone to give up before testing an idea, discount genuine progress or assume that effort is pointless.

If praise and dismissal alternate, someone may become increasingly concerned with predicting other people's reactions instead of assessing their own work. The result can be uncertainty and a need for reassurance rather than better decisions.

## Recognising the Pattern

Look for the difference between a reasoned assessment and an unsupported prediction. "Nobody will use that" followed by specific, relevant customer feedback is potentially useful criticism. The same sentence with no explanation tells you very little. "He's going to be a millionaire" might simply be enthusiasm or a joke, not a serious forecast.

An isolated remark does not establish that anyone is trying to influence you. Context, repetition and concrete attempts to change a decision are more informative than the strength of the wording alone.

## Further considerations
Set realistic goals and decide in advance what would count as useful progress. Break big decisions into small, reversible steps where possible. Test ideas with people who can offer specific feedback, and use actual results rather than confident predictions to guide what happens next.

Do not take major risks because somebody predicts spectacular success. Equally, do not abandon a worthwhile project because somebody dismisses it without reasons.

Remember that even a modest outcome can be valuable. The question is whether your actions achieve something worthwhile on your own terms, not whether they fulfil someone else's forecast.

**A prediction is not an outcome. Judge your actions by evidence, realistic goals and what actually happens.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
