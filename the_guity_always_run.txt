### The Guilty Always Run — Interpreting Avoidance as Evidence of Guilt

This is often quite literally one of their objectives - to get you to literally run away, so they can accuse you of guilt.

**“The guilty always run”** is a psychological manipulation technique in which an individual's attempts to avoid confrontation, intimidation or harassment are presented as evidence that they have something to hide. The objective is to make the victim feel that disengaging, leaving or refusing to participate will be interpreted as an admission of guilt.

The technique exploits **fear of accusation, reputational damage and the natural desire to defend one's innocence**. By portraying avoidance as suspicious, the manipulator attempts to force the victim into continued engagement, even when doing so is distressing, unreasonable or against their interests.

This creates a **double bind**. If the victim walks away, they are accused of running because they are guilty. If they remain and defend themselves, their defensiveness may be presented as evidence of guilt. If they become angry, their emotional reaction may be used against them. Even refusing to respond can be characterised as suspicious.

A particularly coercive variation involves repeatedly provoking someone in public while implying that leaving the situation will confirm an accusation. The objective may be to keep the individual engaged long enough to provoke an emotional outburst, confrontation or other behaviour that can subsequently be used to discredit them.

The underlying psychological trick is to **redefine ordinary self-protective behaviour as incriminating behaviour**, making the victim feel obliged to prove their innocence by complying with the manipulator's demands.

**Countermeasure:** Recognise that disengagement is not evidence of guilt. There is no obligation to remain in an unpleasant or intimidating situation simply because someone threatens to misinterpret your departure. Stay calm, avoid unnecessary confrontation, and continue with whatever you would ordinarily have been doing. Walking away is often the most reasonable response.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
