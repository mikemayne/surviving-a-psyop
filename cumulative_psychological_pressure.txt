# Cumulative Psychological Pressure

**Effects:** Impair judgement, Increase emotional reactivity, Increase distress.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Provoke a reaction.

## The pattern

Not all harmful experiences are dramatic on their own. Repeated criticism, interruptions, ambiguous accusations, uncertainty, humiliating demands, threats and disrupted routines can together consume far more attention than any single event. This is cumulative psychological pressure: the combined burden of having to recover, interpret, prepare and respond again and again. The experiences may have different causes, and they do not need to be part of a single plan for the cumulative strain to be real.

## How it works

Human attention is limited. Every interruption imposes a small cost: assessing what happened, deciding whether it matters, managing emotion and returning to the task at hand. When disruptions are frequent, there is less time to rest, concentrate or maintain relationships. Unpredictability adds another burden because the person starts anticipating the next problem. Fatigue then makes ambiguous situations harder to assess, increasing the temptation to search for explanations that connect everything. The result can become a self-sustaining cycle of vigilance and exhaustion.

## Recognising the pattern

Look at effects rather than trying to settle the cause of every incident. Are sleep, concentration, work, eating, social contact or ordinary enjoyment deteriorating? Is a large part of the day spent reviewing events or trying to prevent another one? It is useful to distinguish verified incidents requiring action from unclear experiences that do not yet justify a conclusion. A stressful pattern deserves support even if its individual elements cannot all be explained.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
