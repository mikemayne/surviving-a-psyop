# Provoking Self-Harm Through False Bodily Claims

**Effects:** Increase distress, Increase emotional reactivity, Increase impulsivity.

**Possible objectives:** Increase the effectiveness of other techniques, Provoke a reaction.

### THE TECHNIQUE

This technique involves telling an individual that something dangerous, invasive or unwanted has been placed inside their body, with the potential effect of encouraging them to injure themselves while attempting to find or remove it.

For example, someone might falsely claim that a listening device, tracking chip or other foreign object has been implanted beneath the skin, perhaps identifying a particular part of the face or body.

The claim creates an apparent problem that the individual may feel compelled to investigate immediately. The mere suggestion can direct attention towards a particular body part and encourage repeated touching, checking or examination.

If the individual accepts the claim, they may attempt increasingly invasive methods of investigation, potentially resulting in serious injury.

### WHY IT WORKS

The technique exploits the natural human tendency to investigate perceived threats to bodily integrity.

Discovering that something had been placed inside your body without your knowledge or consent would ordinarily be alarming. A sufficiently disturbing suggestion may therefore produce an immediate impulse to check, even before its credibility has been evaluated.

This is a form of **action priming**: introducing an idea that makes a particular response more mentally available.

It can also exploit anxiety, uncertainty and the desire to regain control. The individual may begin to interpret ordinary sensations, minor skin irregularities or harmless physical features as possible evidence supporting the original claim.

Repeated checking can then reinforce attention to the supposed problem, creating a cycle in which the absence of evidence fails to provide reassurance.

### RECOGNISING THE PATTERN

The warning sign is an alarming claim about the body that implicitly encourages potentially harmful investigation without providing credible evidence.

Particular caution is warranted when someone claims to possess secret knowledge about an implanted object, insists that medical professionals cannot be trusted, or encourages the individual to investigate or remove something themselves.

The claim may not contain an explicit instruction to self-harm. Instead, the dangerous action is presented as the natural next step in resolving the supposed problem.

This distinction matters: a statement can create a risk of self-injury without establishing that the speaker intended that outcome.

### Further considerations
**Recognise the implied call to action and refuse to act on it.**

An alarming statement about your body is not evidence that anything has been implanted or that intervention is necessary.

Avoid repeatedly examining, scratching, cutting or attempting to remove anything on the strength of another person's unsupported claim.

If there is a genuine physical symptom or concern, seek an ordinary medical assessment rather than attempting to investigate it yourself.

It can also help to identify the specific behaviour the statement appears to encourage. Recognising that a frightening claim is prompting an unnecessary or dangerous action creates an opportunity to interrupt the response.

**The central principle is simple: do not allow someone else's unsupported claims about your body to dictate potentially harmful physical actions.**

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
