How to survive a Psy-op

The techniques you need to survive are all presented in the first page, with the remainder of the site detailing potential techniques and technologies.  I would encourage everyone to read the first page, and present the rest only because knowing how the tricks work significantly decreases their effectiveness.

The techniques described below may be employed intensively and repeatedly over sustained periods.  Most but not all of these tricks involve using surveillance abilities of some kind, typically a hacked mobile phone.  Only audio is needed, although video such as webcams gives a better read of your current emotional state and makes the techniques more effective.

I detail all the techniques here that I have personally experienced, there may be more but they generally fall into one of these categories:

**Incitement to Violence**

This technique involves deliberately provoking an individual into an impulsive or aggressive response through actions designed to create fear, anger or a sense of immediate threat. The goal is to provoke the victim into committing an act of violence, sometimes gradually over the course of several weeks.  The provocation could be a minor illegal act, perhaps harassment, that the police do not have resources to do anything about with the available evidence.  The tactics could be subtle, perhaps negging by strangers in person. Sometimes crimes are committed on cctv footage over the course of a few weeks. There could be direct confrontation or physical provocation.

The technique may be particularly effective when combined with sustained psychological pressure, as accumulated frustration and emotional exhaustion can reduce an individual's ability to respond calmly and proportionately.

**Sleep Deprivation**

The objective of this strategy is to undermine a person's psychological and physical resilience by repeatedly disrupting or preventing normal sleep. Sustained sleep deprivation can cause exhaustion, impaired concentration, emotional instability and reduced ability to think clearly or make decisions. Over time, it may increase vulnerability to stress, confusion and psychological manipulation. The intention is to leave the person increasingly fatigued, distressed and less able to respond effectively to challenging situations or maintain their normal routines.

**Gaslighting**

The objective of this strategy is to make an individual doubt their own reality.  Repeated exposure to the techniques described in this pamphlet can reinforce these associations through familiarity, expectation and emotional salience. Over time, this may produce an automatic, involuntary impression that one's thoughts are being observed or anticipated, even when the individual consciously understands that mind-reading is not possible. The distinction is between rational belief and conditioned psychological response: intellectually rejecting an explanation does not necessarily prevent repeated experiences from triggering an automatic impression consistent with it.

**Emotional Burden**

The objective of this strategy is to impose a sustained psychological burden by repeatedly exposing someone to emotionally distressing statements, accusations or hypotheticals. The intention is not necessarily to convince the person that the statements are true, but to provoke negative emotions, occupy their attention and gradually exhaust their emotional resilience. Even when individual statements are dismissed as absurd or untrue, their cumulative effect may cause distress, fatigue and difficulty concentrating on ordinary activities.

**Button Pushing**

This is a catchall for everything else. It involves making statements designed to provoke a particular emotional response, or get a specific reaction out of an victim.  Learn to recognise a call to action.  Techniques can be combined, and surveillance capabilities make them more effective and are required for some of the tricks to work at all.  It's important to remember that everything they let you hear is on purpose, they may stage conversations that make it seem like you've overheard some super secret plan or that they're worried about going to prison.  If you heard it, its because they want you to.  Treat everything you overhear from an abuser as manipulation.


** How to survive **

There are just four simple techniques you need to know in order to survive:

1. **Stay calm** — If someone makes a provocative, insulting or accusatory remark, you do not have to argue, explain yourself or try to prove them wrong. Disengage and walk away if it is safe to do so. Avoid threatening, chasing or retaliating. This is advice about **not escalating provocation**, not about giving up your right to protect yourself from physical harm or to report an offence. If you are in immediate danger, get to safety and call 999.

2. **Stick to a plan** — Do only what you would do anyway.  If you are feeling completely overwhelmed and don't know what you should do or feel compelled to act, go to bed and stay there until you feel able to react normally.

3. **Don't Assume** — Don't assume anything you are told by an abuser is true. If you are unsure if a voice is coming from your own mouth, make an innocuous noise like a thoughtful clicking or whistle. You will be able to localise that sound and distinguish it from a sound coming from elsewhere.

4. **Don't engage** — If you don't react you don't give them anything to work with.  Record what they say in an email if convenient, then ignore them completely and get on with your day


** How to report **
1. Try to strike a balance between collecting evidence, and maintaining a normal life.  Email yourself descriptions of things you hear, as close to the time they happen as you can.  Don't fall into the trap of thinking someone's accusations make you appear guilty.  You're writing down what they said, not endorsing their statement. Try to get as much sleep as possible, if the choice is collecting evidence or going to sleep choose sleep.
2. As far as possible use email for your correspondence.  It's time stamped, backed up on a remote server and you can connect AI models to it to help you assess replies objectively against your legal rights. 
3. Report experiences promptly, calmly and only report experiences not interpretations. Report the words you heard said, where, and who by if you know.  Don't say what you think was meant, or claim anything that you don't have proof of to be true.  If you are only making accurate statements in good faith you are not wasting police time. 
4. If your reports are dismissed or ignored, politely and calmy escalate until you get a reply.  Sometimes it will be the case that the police cannot help you, based on the evidence you have.  Accept this politely, and keep the emails.
5. Keep going until you are listened to.  The abusers will try to make you think that they're going to give up, or that they will report themselves to relevant authorities if you stop.  They won't, its a delaying tactic. They are likely to wait until it looks like you will give up reporting and then start again.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
