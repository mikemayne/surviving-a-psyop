# Reducing Stress and Sleep Discipline

Stress and sleep deprivation can seriously impair your judgement, concentration and emotional resilience. Looking after your physical and mental wellbeing should be a priority, particularly during difficult or confusing periods.

**Reduce unnecessary stimulation.** Consider disabling all notifications on your phone, including messages, news and social media alerts. You can still check your messages when you choose, rather than allowing other people and applications to constantly interrupt you. Keep essential emergency communications available if necessary.

**Establish a regular sleep routine.** Go to bed and wake up at roughly the same time every day. Keep your bedroom cool, dark and quiet. Avoid caffeine late in the day, and reduce screen use before bedtime.

**Don't fight sleeplessness.** If you cannot sleep, and feel calm get up and do something quiet and relaxing until you feel sleepy. Avoid repeatedly checking the time or worrying about how much sleep you are losing.  If you feel pressured, overwhelmed or unable to decide what to do stay in bed.  Read a book or do something offline to distract yourself if you can.  If you are being pressured to answer questions, or engage with abusers in any way just do your best to ignore them.  Remember any information you give them can be fed into a LLM and used to further harass you, or potentially used to blackmail you or turn people against you.  They can be very hard to ignore, and the panic response can be to answer a question or try to justify your actions.  Resist this urge.  You don't owe them answers.  You don't owe them anything.

**Noise machines** You might be tempted to use noise machines to cover up their activity, and for example to prevent your family from hearing them.  This is not your responsibility.  Use noise machines only if they help you sleep, not to try to cover up the abuse.

**Look after your body.** Regular exercise, daylight, nutritious food and time outdoors can all help regulate stress and improve sleep.

**Give yourself permission to disengage.** You don't need to respond immediately to every message, solve every problem or investigate every unusual occurrence. Set aside time for enjoyable activities and ordinary social contact.

**Seek help when necessary.** Persistent insomnia or overwhelming stress deserves medical attention. Sleep deprivation can make even ordinary difficulties feel much more threatening.

The objective is simple: **protect your sleep, reduce unnecessary demands on your attention, and make important decisions when you are rested.**

**Avoid drugs and alcohol.** Alcohol and recreational drugs can impair judgement, increase anxiety, disrupt sleep and make stressful situations harder to manage. Although they may provide temporary relief, they often make matters worse longterm. If you are experiencing unusual or distressing events, staying sober will help you think clearly, make better decisions and maintain your emotional resilience. If you use alcohol or drugs heavily or regularly, seek medical advice before stopping abruptly, as withdrawal can sometimes be dangerous.
