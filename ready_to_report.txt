# Ready to report?

My advice is to be **polite, fair and calm, but firm and insistent.** Familiarise yourself with your rights, and never feel that you're inconveniencing someone simply by exercising them.

Every communication should aim to achieve one of three outcomes:

1. An answer to your direct questions.
2. A referral to someone who can help.
3. An explanation of why an answer cannot be provided.

If you receive none of these after a couple of reasonable attempts, politely escalate your enquiry to the person's manager or the appropriate complaints team. **Keep going until you get an answer.**

Accept answers with good grace, even when they aren't the answers you wanted. Remain courteous, avoid unnecessary confrontation and stick to the facts.

**Keep copies of everything. Wherever possible, conduct complaints and follow-up correspondence by email.** This gives you a dated record of what you've asked and how people have responded.

## 01 / PREPARE YOUR RECORD

**Write down what actually happened.**

Collect your recollections in a simple text file. For each incident, record:

- What happened, including the exact words spoken if you remember them.
- Where and approximately when it happened.
- Who was present or involved, if known.
- What you personally saw or heard.
- Any witnesses or existing evidence that might help establish the facts.

Be clear when you're uncertain about a detail. Don't fill gaps in your memory with assumptions.

Preserve any original recordings, messages or documents separately.

Email yourself brief factual notes when convenient, creating a dated record. Once something is recorded, put it aside rather than examining it repeatedly. ChatGPT can help you collate these notes into a full report.

## 02 / WRITE YOUR SUMMARY

**Stick to the facts.**

Write a short, chronological account of what you believe has happened, drawing on your incident record.

Describe your direct observations, the effects on your life and the reasons you're concerned. Distinguish what you know from what you suspect.

Avoid accusing people of specific crimes or claiming to know their motives. You can identify people you directly observed without making conclusions about their intentions.

You don't need to identify the offence, explain how everything happened or prove a theory. Let the relevant authorities assess the information.

Keep your summary short and offer the more detailed record separately.

## 03 / TALK TO YOUR GP

**Keep an open mind about your health.**

Remain open to the possibility that some or all of your experiences could have a medical or psychological explanation.

Prepare a list of what you've experienced, how it made you feel, and any effects on your sleep, mood, concentration or everyday life.

Avoid speculating about particular medical conditions or making accusations against other people. Simply describe your experiences as accurately as possible.

Send the list to your GP using your surgery's accepted contact method, then request an appointment to discuss it in person. Ask them to consider your experiences and what support or assessment might be appropriate.

## 04 / CONSIDER THE POLICE

**Find out what might be reportable.**

In parallel with seeking medical advice, you can use ChatGPT or another AI assistant to help identify whether any specific events you've experienced might constitute a criminal offence, including harassment, threats or intimidation.

Ask it to consider relevant UK law, ordinary alternative explanations and what information the police would need to assess your concerns.

Avoid sharing unnecessary identifying or sensitive information with an AI service.

## 05 / MAKE YOUR REPORT

**Use the appropriate channels.**

For non-emergency police matters in the UK, contact your local police force through its official online reporting service or another accepted written channel.

Written communication is preferable wherever practical: it provides a dated record of exactly what you reported and how the police responded.

If someone is in immediate danger, call **999**. Don't delay an urgent report to prepare written evidence.

Give the police your factual summary, explain your concerns and offer any supporting information you have. You do not need to finish collecting evidence before reporting a possible crime.

Ask for a reference number and keep copies of all correspondence.

If you don't receive a response, follow up politely. After a couple of reasonable attempts, consider escalating through the appropriate supervisory or complaints channels.

Remember the three possible outcomes: an answer, a referral or a clear explanation of why an answer isn't available. You may not always receive the outcome you hoped for, but you can reasonably ask for your concerns to be addressed.

## 06 / GET ON WITH YOUR LIFE

**Protect your wellbeing.**

Try to strike a balance between reporting your experiences and maintaining your ordinary life.

Don't confront people, attempt your own investigation or feel obliged to respond to provocative behaviour.

Get enough sleep, continue your normal activities and stay in contact with appropriate support.

Review your records periodically rather than continually. Obsessively examining every detail can increase distress without improving the quality of your report.

**Once you've reported what you know, you've taken a constructive step. You don't have to solve everything yourself.**

---

*This booklet is not a medical, legal or police reporting service. Its purpose is to help you organise your experiences and approach the appropriate professionals calmly and constructively.*
