# Deprivation of Basic Necessities as Punishment

## The Technique

Withholding food, water, sleep, hygiene, necessary medical attention, communication aids or access to a toilet can become a means of coercion when basic needs are made conditional on obedience. The deprivation need not be dramatic to be damaging: repeated delays, arbitrary restrictions or threats that care will be withdrawn may be enough to make someone feel helpless. The crucial issue is whether access to an essential need is being misused to compel behaviour rather than managed for a legitimate, proportionate reason.

## Why It Works

Basic needs cannot simply be ignored. Hunger, thirst, pain, exhaustion and physical discomfort narrow attention to immediate relief. In that state a person may comply with demands they would normally refuse. The imbalance becomes especially severe when someone is ill, detained, disabled, very young, or otherwise dependent on a caregiver. Even the uncertainty of not knowing whether a need will be met can make a person anxious about challenging the person in control.

## Recognising the Pattern

Distinguish a genuine practical constraint from punitive withholding. A delay caused by staffing pressures or an appropriate clinical restriction is not necessarily abuse. A pattern becomes more concerning when the restriction is accompanied by threats, humiliation, unrelated demands, inconsistent explanations or retaliation for asking questions. Keep the description concrete: what was needed, what was requested, how long access was delayed, and what explanation was given.

## Further considerations
State the immediate need plainly and without argument. If a person responsible for care refuses, ask for another responsible professional, a senior member of staff, or an independent advocate. Keep a factual record of serious incidents where feasible. If there is an immediate medical risk, seek urgent medical assistance. Avoid allowing access to essential care to become a bargain over unrelated behaviour. Everyone's fundamental needs deserve to be addressed on their own merits.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
