# Manufactured Moral Justification

**Effects:** Increase shame, Increase self-doubt, Increase distress.

**Possible objectives:** Make mistreatment appear deserved, Discourage complaints or challenges, Provoke a defensive reaction.

## The pattern

A possible manipulation tactic is to loudly discuss something a person supposedly did in the past, then react with extraordinary anger, disgust or moral outrage. The discussion implies that whatever treatment the person is receiving now must be justified.

The past event could be real, exaggerated, taken out of context or entirely invented. It might have no meaningful connection to the present situation.

For example, imagine several people talking within earshot of someone who is being treated badly:

- "Did you hear what he did years ago?"
- "That's absolutely disgusting!"
- "After that, I've got no sympathy for him."
- "Well, he deserves whatever he gets."

No one has actually established that the allegation is true, or explained why it would justify the present behaviour. Instead, the strength of the reaction invites the listener to supply the missing argument.

## How it works

**Retroactive justification:** The conversation presents an alleged past wrong as an explanation for current treatment, even where no valid connection has been established.

**Emotional substitution:** Outrage is used in place of evidence or reasoning. The stronger the reaction, the more serious the accusation can appear.

**Implied guilt:** The speakers discuss the allegation as though it has already been proved, without showing that it has.

**Apparent social agreement:** Several people reacting together can make condemnation seem widely shared, whether or not they have independent knowledge.

**Self-blame and diversion:** The person may start defending their past or wondering whether they deserve mistreatment, rather than assessing the current behaviour on its own merits.

The unsupported leap is simple: **something bad is alleged about a person, therefore mistreating that person now is acceptable.**

Even if a past wrongdoing is established, it doesn't automatically justify unrelated or disproportionate treatment.

## What makes this distinct

This is different from simply saying "we only do this to really bad people." Here, the apparent justification is *performed*: a conversation about the past, conspicuous moral outrage and implied agreement encourage someone else to make the connection.

An overheard discussion, genuine anger or strong moral criticism does not by itself show that the speakers are addressing anyone nearby or deliberately manipulating them. Context, clear references, repetition and independent evidence matter before drawing conclusions about intent.

## Response

Keep two questions separate: **Did the alleged past event happen?** And **would it justify the treatment being discussed?** Neither is answered by how loudly someone expresses disgust.

You do not have to defend yourself against an ambiguous overheard remark. If there is a direct accusation or a specific incident of mistreatment, address the facts through an appropriate process rather than trying to decode a conversation.

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
