# Dont Do It Yourself

**Effects:** Pressure toward harmful decisions.

**Possible objectives:** Induce self-harm or unlawful reactions.

The difference between psychological abuse and a psyop is in my opinion the goal to make someone do someting to themself that the abusers can't necessarily do directly.  That does not mean the abusers don't enjoy the process.

The goal might be imprisonment, or suicide.  Other possible goals include silencing, discrediting or controlling someone who possesses sensitive information, influence or something else of value.

In my opinion, **secrecy and plausible deniability** would be the overriding priorities of anyone conducting this kind of abuse. Ideally, from their perspective, the target appears to have acted entirely of their own free will, leaving little evidence of external involvement.  If evidence is left of involvement, it is designed to be either deniable or insignificant compared to the coerced actions of the victim.

**The practical advice is simple: don't do their work for them.**

Don't hurt yourself, threaten anyone, commit a crime or make irreversible decisions in response to perceived provocation. Don't accept the idea that you must act to make something stop.

You retain control over your own decisions, whatever somebody else's intentions might be. Protect that independence. Disengage, seek appropriate support when needed, and carry on with your life.

## Response

**Don't engage at all. Record if convenient, then get on with your day.**

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, get to safety and call **999**. Seeking emergency help is not engaging with a provocation.

Read [the Golden Rule chapter](https://mikemayne.github.io/surviving-a-psyop/#read/never_engage).

**If you don't respond, they have nothing to work with.**

**If you don't react, you don't give them anything to work with.**
